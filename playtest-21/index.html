Playtest 21
Patch Notes

PLAYTEST
21

Playtest 20 gave you a lot to play with, and a lot to read. You told us the BRIEFING had become an essay, that every card wanted a paragraph before you could pick it, and that the works vote on top of everything else was too much going on. This build fixes the reading. The BRIEFING is four plain questions with the answers under them and one figure for what the run pays. Every card says what it does in nine words or fewer and shows the rest as marks, with the full rule a hold away. A break asks one question at most, answered one way: hold Tab, tap 1 or 2. The works build themselves. Nothing else in the game does anything different; it just tells you so faster.

Recut on 27 October, after your first session. Everything you told us that afternoon that could be fixed the same day is in this build: the fight’s HUD is trimmed, Y is a tap, the break is five seconds longer, the card hand-off is gone, the medic can plant the Aid Post before a wave, and the range, the after-action card and the names are fixed. Overnight, the bigger asks landed too: the Quartermaster sends his Issue without a second pick, the card draw stops dealing ammo twice, and a man you shoot from outside the wire comes for you. The day after, the rest of what you asked for: you choose where the works go, the break reads in its nineteen seconds, the ace at the end is a boss and the road to the lorries can be cut, and you get into the lorries yourself or get left on the line. Details under From your first session, Overnight and The day after. No card changed what it does. Your save loads as it is. Everyone in a co-op game needs this build.

From your first session

Recut
  • Less on the screen in a fight. The column under the wave count now carries only what you can act on in the next half minute. The section roster, the dispatch tracker and the pay line are break reading (hold Tab for the section at any time). The rules tags stay for the first five seconds of a wave, the main thrust for ten (the compass rim keeps it all wave), PERFECT WAVE folds to one line after six seconds and leaves once it’s lost. A counter-attack is two lines, and a map case lying by its rider is one mark, not two.
  • Tap Y for the whole rule. A tap opens every card to its full rule and they stay open until you tap again or pick. Holding still peeks, as before.
  • Five seconds more with the cards. A plain break is 19 s, not 14. A question still holds it open to its clock.
  • No more handing a card to a mate. It asked a key of you in the one place you were already reading. X only gives a field find to the nearest mate now, and the PLAYTEST 21 card says so.
  • The medic plants the Aid Post before a wave. Hold E in the break to plant it for the wave to come; a tap still readies you. It stands through that wave and uses one of the wave’s two charges.
  • A crosshair of your own: cross or dot, white, green or amber, 60 to 160%, under OPTIONS › GAMEPLAY.
  • Names the game can draw. A name of symbols or another script drew as a row of bars on both rosters and made the lobby re-introduce that soldier every second. Names are kept to letters now, 20 at most; a name with nothing left falls back to “Rifleman N”.

Overnight: the bigger asks

Recut
  • The Quartermaster sends, you don’t pick. Every third wave held still brings an Issue, but a second into the break it arrives on its own: one item for the section, decided by what the section needs (the post under 60% or a gap in the ring gets a Sandbag Detail, men fallen get Fresh Riflemen, a doctrine in force gets Captured Orders, else something for the wave to come such as a Sniper Team or a Mine Belt), and one item for you, down a printed ladder (empty pouches get a Grenade Belt; then your class card; then ammunition, dressing, grenades, a satchel, the medic’s bag; then a ration or smoke; then a fire mission). One banner, one toast, no second strip, no “2 TO PICK”. The chit re-rolls commendations only.
  • One ammo card an offer, at most. You told us every perk wanted to give you ammo back. Twelve cards in a hundred do, but the draw was dealing them on four offers in ten and two together on one in thirteen. The gun slot no longer climbs to the next tier when yours is empty, a second ammo card is swapped for one of the same tier and school, and the eight cards that all said “a headshot kill puts a round back” are one rule now: ONE ROUND BACK and Thumbed Round keep it and print the cap (two a kill). Stripper Clips is a magazine card, Coastwatcher’s Map gets you spotted later, the Marksman’s eye hits harder, Officer’s Sidearm trades its rounds for damage and less kick, and the rifleman’s Loose Rounds is Field Glasses: a headshot kill marks the men around him for the section. Doubles went from one offer in thirteen to one in nine hundred. The Grease Gun’s Flash Hider is a Basic now, not a Rare.
  • They answer you. A man you shoot from outside the wire shoots back, and his squad comes for you: for twenty seconds, standing off at fifteen to twenty metres, giving up when you go quiet or get back inside. Hits count, near misses don’t. At most four squads in ten hunt at once, so the post is still pressed; charges, sappers, officers, snipers and gun crews don’t leave their job. A section that holds the wire sees no change.
  • Going out pays, a little. A kill made past the wire with the post at half or better is a BEYOND THE WIRE citation (200 Spirit for the first in a wave, 40 after), and the accolade pays 10 RP on your third and ninth such kill, or at once for an officer, sniper or gun crew, twice a wave at most. Every citation now says what it was for (“OVER THE TOP, caught coming over the wall”). Counter-attacks come from wave 3, every second or third wave.
  • The field glasses tell the truth. GUNS SILENT in a Counter-Battery wave, FIRE MISSION INBOUND while shells are on their way, the kills you’re short otherwise; never a full meter beside NOT READY.
  • HQ loads with fewer hitches, and the F10 report now says what the slowest frame was doing, whether the window had focus, where you stood and what was under your crosshair, so the next “this prop” report can be found.

The day after: works, bosses, the lorries

Recut
  • Work this side. In a works break, look at a side of the post and hold F for half a second: the pioneer sergeant puts the next piece the rule allows on that side, at once. Eight sides, read from where you look; the first hold wins and the others get a toast with the name. Nobody holding for twelve seconds gives the rule’s piece as before. A gate cannot be shut (the squad and the lorries need the way), so looking into one is refused and the prompt says to look out past it.
  • WIRE THE GATE. From wave 6 the pioneers can string concertina across a gateway. The gap stays open, but infantry inside it move at less than half speed: a trooper spends a second longer in the coils under the gate’s gun, a rifleman three. Your own men and the lorries pass at full speed. One a gate.
  • The break reads in nineteen seconds. The column under the wave count carries what the next wave asks of you, one line per thing: intelligence in three lines, recon in one, the works in one, the dispatch in one. A section of four or more lists name and class only, and the RP is a column on the Tab roll call. The pay line shows on a run’s first break and when the pay changes. Teaching lines stay for a career’s first three breaks, then move to the field manual. A six-man break went from 24 lines to 16, and at HUD 130% it no longer runs into the squad list.
  • The ace is a boss. In the final assault the enemy ace comes last, is named in the break before, and only his weak points count until he is crippled: the deck, the sides, the running gear; a mine under his belly throws a track. Crippled, everything counts. His bounty is half again, and killing him with the post above half pins GIANT KILLER. Solo, the AT crate’s five rockets kill a Panzer ace with one to spare and a Tiger ace with none; the gun’s AP and satchels are the margin.
  • THE ARMOURED TRAIN. On Factory District’s rail yard the final assault’s last act is a Panzerzug: it steams in on the far track, halts broadside sixty metres out and is fought car by car. Rifles on the flak crew at the nose, rockets on the turret and the boiler (the plate takes nothing until the boiler is blown), then the magazine car for the kill. Three times it puts a company off onto the field. The relief column comes up past the wreck. Solo it costs six of the crate’s eleven rockets; at six players fourteen of thirty-six.
  • THE ROAD IS CUT. In HOLD THE LINE, when the lorries are called on a German front, Hauptmann Hartkopf, “Der Riegel”, brings a King Tiger down the road with the surge and halts past the evacuation point. The lorries will not set off while he stands: the clock reads ROAD CUT. Throw his track (two rockets, or one satchel) and they come at once; CLEAR ROAD pays for opening it inside their minute. Still standing at ninety seconds, the road stayed cut and the line goes on. On the Japanese fronts it is Taisa Hayashida’s charge instead: the Colonel on horseback with his colours, four sabres, a Chi-Ha and a banzai behind. Shoot the horse and he is on foot by the road, sword out, sending every man within thirty metres in every fourteen seconds. Cut him down, or cripple the Chi-Ha, and the road opens.
  • Get into the lorries, or get left on the line. The lorries drive in, swing round and park facing out with the tailgates to the post. F at a tailgate seats you, rifle live: four to a bed, and a ring-mounted .30 cal on the lead cab for one man (F at the cab step). They leave thirty seconds after arriving, or five seconds after the last living soldier is aboard. A man down within three metres of a tailgate is hauled in. A man not aboard is LEFT ON THE LINE: no evacuation pay, no decoration, his name on everyone’s telegram. Those aboard ride out firing for twelve seconds, and the squad climbs in behind the first of you.
  • Gone: the MG nest counter-attack. Shooting the crew on the way in paid as a win; rather than tune it, the nest is gone. The mortar pit, the dispatches, the crossroads and each front’s own job stay, and they come from wave 3 now.
  • The squad was never idle. The four men standing round a tin at the post were HQ’s brew-up scenery, put into the field on co-op clients by a deployment bug. Fixed, and while we were there: followers no longer stand in a knot, a man kneeling with an enemy in plain view now fires, and a client sees every one of the host’s squad shots.
  • Smaller: the AT crate warns “you’ll leave the flamethrower” and leaves it at your feet to take back; THROW IT BACK reaches 3 m and half a second; DONATE repeats while held and DONATE ALL asks once; the field glasses say GUNS SILENT, FIRE MISSION INBOUND or the kills you’re short, never a full meter beside NOT READY; the Grease Gun’s Flash Hider is a Basic.

The BRIEFING answers four questions

Changed
  • The left column is four fields, always in the same place: WHERE · NEXT DEPLOYMENT, WHICH RUN, HOW HARD · WHAT IT PAYS, and YOUR KIT. Each one is a caption, the answer in a line or two, and the button that changes it. Your kit sits at the same height whatever rank you are.
  • WHERE names the front, says whether it’s SECURED, how many waves win it and your best, and gives the first line of its story. CHANGE FRONT is at the right where it always was.
  • The board’s rows of A / B / C, OP, OR with its arrows and “1/7”, CMP and the warning lines are gone from the page. One field says which run you’re on; CHANGE RUN opens the rest.
  • “ORDERS FROM HQ” is gone from the dispatches, and the board’s good surprises are called a GIFT, not an anomaly.
  • Nothing on the page is smaller than 14 pt any more. A line that would have shrunk to fit is cut short instead.

Which run: one field and one sheet

New
  • The WHICH RUN field is three lines: the run’s letter and a plain title (C · THE HARDEST RUN, WEEKLY OPERATION, DAILY ORDERS, CHALLENGE, THE CAMPAIGN), its modifiers as a pip and a name each, and what the pips mean. Until you’ve taken a dealt run, line three says “two harder runs pay more: CHANGE RUN”.
  • CHANGE RUN opens the RUN sheet. TODAY’S BOARD is three cards side by side: letter, title, what it pays, and a line per modifier with its effect. The one you’re on says SELECTED. Under them, OTHER WAYS TO PLAY lists the weekly operation, the daily orders, the campaign and each challenge as a row you can take.
  • THE MAP block prints the next deployment’s conditions the same way: a pip and a name each, in ink, instead of names joined with a plus sign in red.
  • In a session a client’s field shows the host’s pick and says “the host picks”, with SEE THE BOARD to read it.

One figure for what a run pays

Changed
  • HOW HARD · WHAT IT PAYS gives one total in red (“pays RP x3.00 · XP x1.90”) and the parts under it in whole percents: “Standard tier · 2 handicaps +55% · Pact heat 9 +72% · run C +44%”. The same total is on the deploy bar, at the foot of the difficulty sheet and in the TEAM header, and it is exact: it’s the figure your run pays at wave 1.
  • The difficulty sheet’s tabs read TIER / HANDICAPS / THE PACT, and every figure on them is a part of the total (“+55% RP”, “pays as is”, “heat 9 · +72% RP”). When the cap bites the sheet says so and what it caps.
  • The field is up whenever there is a difficulty to speak of, and the CHANGE button only when there’s a dial to set. Before you’ve secured a front, the run’s pay is changed with CHANGE RUN.
  • A new soldier sees a Replacements line instead of a difficulty, and a client sees the host’s pick.

Cards you can read in two seconds

Changed
  • One line, nine words at most. Every one of the 171 cards (commendations, veteran cards and the Quartermaster’s Issue) has a hand-written face line: the trigger first, the number last. “Headshot kill -> a round back into the gun.” The old paragraph is still there, one hold away.
  • Marks instead of sentences. The school is a lettered badge on the card’s foot (M, S, CQ, D, F, SG, +) with pips: ink pips are the cards you hold of that school, the brass pip is this card, and a brass ring means it brings a set rule in. Beside the name: a gold seal for a rule-breaker, an arrow box for a transform, II for a Rank II copy, stack bars, gold pair brackets that close when you hold the partner, a struck badge for REPLACES, a red diamond for a cursed crate. Gun cards carry the gun in a blue box; class cards a square with the class’s initial.
  • One brass tag at most over the top edge: COMPLETES a pair, SET: a rule it brings in, or FROM a mate.
  • Bigger type. The name is 22 pt, the face 18, nothing on a card under 16 bar the gun’s box. Nothing shrinks to fit any more.
  • A Rank II copy’s face says only what the second rank adds. The seven school colours were re-mixed so they all tell apart for colour-blind players, and the letters say it anyway.
  • Five long names were shortened: Q.M.’s Favourite, Regimental Colours, Fresh Riflemen, Loose Rounds, Mule-Borne Ammo, and Hand-Loaded Ammo. Same cards, same ids.

Tap Y for the whole rule

New
  • Tap Y on the cards and every card opens to its full rule, then a line per mark explaining it (“SET  Close Quarters 3/3 -> COLD STEEL: …”). They stay open until you tap again or pick. Hold it for 0.8 s instead and they open only while it’s held (the recut: the first build was hold only). You can pick at any time, open or not.
  • 28 of the full rules were rewritten with the numbers the old text left out: Belt Swap’s 8 s, Covering Fire’s 1 s linger, Shock Rounds’ odds. Every safety clause and cooldown is in the hold.
  • A one-time caption on your first offer says “Tap [Y]: the full rule”.

The strip

Changed
  • The card strip follows HUD SIZE now, not MENU TEXT. It keeps clear of the compass, the weapon corner and the crosshair. Five or six cards come in two rows at full size instead of one squeezed row.
  • One header line (“FIELD COMMENDATION · 1-3”, or “[Y] + 1-3” in a fight), short reroll and chit lines, and a PITY +20% tag. The “No hurry: it waits into the next wave” line shows on your first three offers only.
  • The world dims behind the strip in a break, and the number keys arm the moment the break starts.
  • Mates’ picks: “DAN took HOT STREAK II” with the school badge, under the kill feed for 3 s, on every machine. It replaces the host’s old “X took LEGENDARY” chat alert. Pairs completed and section sets still post their gold chat alerts.

The codex, the dossier and the Tab sheet

Changed
  • RECORDS › CODEX shows every card’s face in the grid and the full card on the right with its source, state and odds. DRAW RULES opens on THE MARKS, a key to every symbol, once after this update.
  • The commendation dossier at HQ shows the real cards; rest the mouse on one for its full rule.
  • The Tab sheet shows mini cards with the badge, and the ringed card’s box shows its face and then the rule. Set rows are badge, pips and the rule’s name.
  • RULES IN FORCE leads each tag with its badge, seal or pair mark.

The works build themselves

Changed
  • No vote. From rank 8 the section still earns pioneers from waves held (one a wave, one more per 15 kills). Every third break the pioneer sergeant puts up the piece the ground asks for, facing where recon says the next wave comes from, and it stands for the whole deployment.
  • The rule is printed, not voted: a listening post before wave 5 if the pioneers can pay for it; otherwise wire, then a breastwork, then a pit, then the parapet – the first one that side hasn’t got and the pioneers can pay for. When nothing can be sited or paid for, the pioneers are kept for next time.
  • NEXT BREAK names it a break ahead (“THE WORKS – wire, west”), and the banner says what went up. The works panel is one line in a quiet break and keeps off the screen while a question is up; since they ask nothing, the works may go up in a question break too, and only the march keeps them off.
  • LANE INTEL and WORKS on RULES IN FORCE stay as they were. K does nothing.

One question a break

New
  • Hold Tab, tap 1 or 2. Push on, a section dare, the route, the campaign’s fork, the lorries and HOLD THE LINE? all ask the same way now. 1 is the first answer (push on, take the dare, route A, call the lorries); 2 is the second (route B), or tap your answer again to take it back. Let go of Tab and nothing has changed.
  • One panel, where the ready panel was: the question, its two answers with their terms, every soldier’s answer as it comes in, the clock, and in brass what happens if nobody answers (“Nobody: the break as usual, mend and make ready”). Leaving it is always fine.
  • One question a break, never two. A route or fork vote is 12 s, not 20, and closes 3 s after everyone has answered. Push on is 8 s, a dare 10 s, the lorries 20 s.
  • E only readies you (and repairs the post, held, if you’re the engineer). Holding E to push on, as in playtest 20, is gone. Solo, Tab + 1 pushes on and a tap of E declines it and starts the wave.
  • With a card offer up, Tab + 1 answers and picks no card; 1 alone picks a card and answers nothing.

Together

Co-op
  • The TEAM header names the run’s total (“HOSTING · pays RP x3.00”) instead of the Pact heat, so it’s the same figure as the BRIEFING and the bar. Nothing is added when the run pays as is.
  • The host’s line says what the host decides: the front, the run and the difficulty for everyone, and DEPLOY never waits for ready ticks. Roster names fit before they shrink, and your own folds “(you)” to “·you” when it must.
  • A client’s BRIEFING fields show the host’s choices and say “the host picks” where the buttons would be.
  • The field manual’s first lobby note now says: pick a gun and a specialist under LOADOUT, then MARK READY; the host picks the run.

Keys

Changed
  • Tap Y on a card offer for the full rule and a line per mark; tap again to close. U rerolls where you can.
  • J, K and L do nothing now. None of them was in reach of the hands that move and aim.
  • Give a find you’re looking at to a mate with X in a wave. X is also the rifle grenade’s key: a press that gives doesn’t load the launcher, every other press does. (The recut removed the card hand-off that shared this key.)
  • Your field kit is on Z, beside your movement hand. Ping is on Q alone (Z was its second key).
  • Hold F looking at a side in a works break to put the works there; at a lorry’s tailgate to get aboard; at the lead cab’s step to man its .30 cal.
  • Hold Tab and tap 1 or 2 to answer the section’s question. A tap of E readies you; hold-E to push on, from playtest 20, is gone. A medic’s hold of E in a break plants the Aid Post (the engineer’s hold repairs, as before).
  • All rebindable at OPTIONS › CONTROLS, which has one row fewer.

Your save

Changed
  • Nothing changes in your save. Every card, kit, boon and level loads as it was, and no card does anything different.
  • A key you rebound yourself is kept. If your own ping binding kept Z, your field kit comes up with no key rather than sharing it: the WHAT’S NEW card says so once, and OPTIONS › CONTROLS sets one.
  • A soldier with offers behind them never sees the “it waits” line; a new soldier sees it three times. The codex’s THE MARKS page opens itself once.
  • Everyone in a co-op game needs playtest 21. The mates’ picks line rides the wire, so an older build gets a plain message to update instead of a silent drop.

Fixes

Fixed
  • The recut: a RECON panel from a run you’d left in a break sat over the range course’s station card (and would have named a false main thrust on your next first wave). Recon’s bearings are forgotten when a run ends, and the wave column never draws on the range.
  • The recut: the range’s marker sat on the target’s chest with its distance under it, hiding a 50 m target. It sits above the head now, names a place to get to with the distance (“THE PEG – 21 M”), and the objective is a diamond on the compass too.
  • The recut: on a client’s after-action card, soldiers who had left paired up with the wrong rows (two soldiers shown twice with each other’s kills and accolades). Each soldier is matched by his machine now, and a soldier who leaves keeps his own figures.
  • The recut: the M1903A4’s Lyman peep ring and front blade took the rifle’s camo, so a snow-white sight vanished against night fog and read as inaccurate. They stay dark steel. The aim itself was true.
  • The BRIEFING, the bar and the TEAM header all print the same pay. Before, a host on run C could read “pays RP x1.78” on the bar while the page showed nothing.
  • At HUD 130% the three longest held cards lost their last line; a mark line gives way first now. At MENU TEXT 130% the codex and dossier grew each wrapped line on its own; they don’t.
  • A number no longer breaks from its unit at a line end (“8 / m”).
  • Three or more cards at HUD 130% can’t fit between the HUD’s corners on 16:9, so the strip steps down to 115% or 100% and never narrows a card. The squad roster and the STEADYING label fold away under it as a last resort.
  • Reduce motion (OPTIONS › weapon sway and bob off) now also stills the Legendary gleam.
  • The first-time “two harder runs pay more” hint only shows while run A is picked; once you’ve found the sheet it gives the legend instead.

Known issues

  • Holding Y on a two-row strip in a fight covers the crosshair while it’s held.
  • On 4:3 and 16:10 screens with HUD 130% and five or six cards, the strip may touch the compass or the Spirit meter; the dossier on a 4:3 screen cuts long names.
  • The glyph letters on the school badges are small at 720p. Tell us if you can’t read one.
  • The BRIEFING’s layout, the pay field’s rule and the works’ cadence of three were set for this playtest and may move after it.
  • The recut’s changes were checked by machines and by the first session’s reports, not played again by a person before it went out. The boss fights and the lorries are the two to try first: their timings come from scripted shots, not from people.

What we’d like you to test

  • Open a card offer and pick without holding Y. Did the face line and the marks tell you enough? Which card made you hold?
  • Did the marks make sense before you read THE MARKS in the codex? Which one didn’t?
  • On the BRIEFING, could you tell where you were going, which run you were on and what it paid without opening anything? Did the one pay figure match what you got?
  • Did you find CHANGE RUN, and did the RUN sheet’s cards read better than the old rows?
  • The section’s question: did you find Tab + 1 without being told? Did you ever pick a card when you meant to answer, or answer when you meant to pick? Did the brass “Nobody:” line make it fine to leave it?
  • The works: did you notice them go up, and did NEXT BREAK’s line tell you where to look? Does the base feel like it builds itself, or barely builds at all?
  • The recut: with the column trimmed, could you read the fight? Did you miss the section roster in a fight, or find it on Tab? Is 19 s enough with the cards? Did a tap of Y in a fight ever get in your way?
  • Medics: did the Aid Post go down in the break on a hold of E, and did a tap still ready you?
  • Overnight: did the Quartermaster’s item make sense for what the section needed, and did you miss picking it? Did the offers still feel like ammo, ammo, ammo? Go out past the wire and shoot a squad: did they come for you, and did the post hold while they did?
  • The day after: did you find hold F for the works without being told, and did the piece go where you meant? Did the wired gate feel closed enough? Was the ace a fight or a chore, and could you tell where to hit him? In HOLD THE LINE, did everyone get aboard, and did the man left on the line feel fairly treated?
  • Did X ever load your rifle grenade when you meant to give a find, or the other way round? Is the field kit easier to reach on Z?
  • In a section, did the mates’ picks line tell you anything useful? Did the TEAM header’s pay match the host’s BRIEFING?
  • Did anything on a card, the strip or the BRIEFING get cut off or overlap at your resolution and HUD size? Say which.
  • Press F10 the moment something looks wrong; it sends the map code for you.
