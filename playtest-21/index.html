Playtest 21
Patch Notes

PLAYTEST
21

Playtest 20 gave you a lot to play with and a lot to read. You told us the BRIEFING had turned into an essay. Every card wanted a paragraph before you could pick it, and the works vote on top of all that was too much. So this build is about reading. The BRIEFING is four plain questions, with the answers under them and one figure for what the run pays. Every card says what it does in nine words or fewer. The rest is shown as marks, and the full rule is one key away. A break asks one question at most, and you always answer it the same way: hold Tab, tap 1 or 2. The works build themselves. Apart from that, the game plays as it did.

Recut on 27 October, after your first session. Everything you raised that afternoon that we could fix the same day is in. The fight’s HUD is trimmed. Y is a tap. Breaks are five seconds longer. The card hand-off is gone. The medic can plant the Aid Post before a wave. The range, the after-action card and the names are fixed. Overnight we did the bigger ones. The Quartermaster sends his Issue without a second pick, the card draw stops dealing ammo twice, and a man you shoot from outside the wire now comes for you. The day after, we did the rest. You choose where the works go. There’s less to read in a break, so it fits in the 19 seconds. The ace at the end is a boss, and the enemy can cut the road to the lorries. You have to get into the lorries yourself now, or you’re left on the line. Details are under From your first session, Overnight and The day after. No card changed what it does, apart from the ammo cards overnight and Chain Reaction on the 28th. Your save loads as it is. Everyone in a co-op game needs this build.

From your first session

Recut
  • Less on the screen in a fight. The column under the wave count only shows what you can act on in the next half minute. The section roster, the dispatch tracker and the pay line have moved to the break. Hold Tab if you want the section mid-fight. The rules tags show for the first five seconds of a wave. The main thrust shows for ten, and the compass rim keeps it for the whole wave. PERFECT WAVE folds to one line after six seconds and goes once you’ve lost it. A counter-attack takes two lines. A map case lying by its rider is one mark now, not two.
  • Tap Y for the whole rule. One tap opens every card to its full rule. They stay open until you tap again or pick. Holding still peeks, like before.
  • Five more seconds with the cards. A plain break is 19 s now, up from 14. A break with a question still stays open until the question’s clock runs out.
  • No more handing a card to a mate. It was one more key to think about, right when you were reading the cards. X now only gives a field find to the nearest mate. The PLAYTEST 21 card says so too.
  • The medic can plant the Aid Post before a wave. Hold E in the break to plant it for the coming wave. A tap still readies you. The Aid Post stands through that wave and uses one of the wave’s two charges.
  • You can change the crosshair now. Cross or dot, white, green or amber, 60 to 160%, under OPTIONS › GAMEPLAY.
  • Names fixed. A name made of symbols or another script showed up as a row of bars on both rosters, and the lobby re-introduced that soldier every second. Names are kept to letters now, 20 at most. If there’s nothing left after that, you’re “Rifleman N”.

Same evening, after Brenton played it

  • Shoot a sapper and his mates go with him. His charge now kills the men beside him, and the kills are yours. A heavy gunner survives it. It does the same damage to you as before. Only his own side dies.
  • Chain Reaction no longer hurts your side. Its belt grenade never hurts you or your squad. It goes off at most once a second per soldier, and a headshot kill inside that second is just a kill. The belt counts as one of your grenades now, so Bundled Charges, Fire Bombs, Demolitions and the rest go off with it.
  • THROW IT BACK has no cut-off. You can grab any live enemy grenade within 3 m, and it goes back with whatever fuse it has left. There’s still a 0.3 s fumble on the grab, so if you’re too late it goes off in your hand.
  • A tap of Y in a fight keeps the cards open now. In the recut’s first hour they shut again straight away.

Overnight: the bigger asks

Recut
  • The Quartermaster sends his Issue. You don’t pick it. You still get an Issue every third wave held, but now it just arrives a second into the break. There’s one item for the section, chosen by what the section needs. If the post is under 60% or there’s a gap in the ring, that’s a Sandbag Detail. If men have fallen, Fresh Riflemen. If a doctrine is in force, Captured Orders. Otherwise it’s something for the coming wave, such as a Sniper Team or a Mine Belt. Your own item comes down a printed ladder. Empty pouches get a Grenade Belt first. Then your class card. Then ammunition, dressing, grenades, a satchel, the medic’s bag. Then a ration or smoke, and last a fire mission. You get one banner and one toast. There’s no second strip and no “2 TO PICK”. The chit re-rolls commendations only.
  • One ammo card an offer, at most. You told us every perk wanted to give you ammo back. Twelve cards in a hundred do, but the draw was dealing them on four offers in ten, and two together on one in thirteen. Now the gun slot doesn’t climb to the next tier when yours is empty, and a second ammo card gets swapped for one of the same tier and school. Eight cards all said “a headshot kill puts a round back”, and that’s one rule now. ONE ROUND BACK and Thumbed Round keep it and print the cap (two a kill). Others changed. Stripper Clips is a magazine card. Coastwatcher’s Map gets you spotted later. The Marksman’s eye hits harder. Officer’s Sidearm trades its rounds for damage and less kick. The rifleman’s Loose Rounds is now Field Glasses: a headshot kill marks the men around him for the section. Doubles went from one offer in thirteen to one in nine hundred. The Grease Gun’s Flash Hider is a Basic now, not a Rare.
  • They come for you. Shoot a man from outside the wire and he shoots back, and his squad comes after you. They hunt you for 20 seconds, standing off at 15 to 20 m, and give up when you go quiet or get back inside. Hits count. Near misses don’t. At most four squads in ten hunt at once, so the post still gets pressed. Charges, sappers, officers, snipers and gun crews stick to their jobs. If your section holds the wire, nothing changes.
  • Going out pays, a little. A kill past the wire with the post at half or better is a BEYOND THE WIRE citation: 200 Spirit for the first in a wave, 40 after that. The accolade pays 10 RP on your third and ninth such kill, or straight away for an officer, sniper or gun crew. It pays twice a wave at most. Every citation now says what it was for (“OVER THE TOP, caught coming over the wall”). Counter-attacks start at wave 3 and come every second or third wave.
  • The field glasses tell the truth. They say GUNS SILENT in a Counter-Battery wave and FIRE MISSION INBOUND while shells are on the way. Otherwise they show the kills you’re short. They never show a full meter beside NOT READY.
  • HQ loads with fewer hitches. The F10 report now says what the slowest frame was doing, whether the window had focus, where you were standing and what was under your crosshair. Next time someone reports “this prop”, we can find it.

The day after: works, bosses, the lorries

Recut
  • Work this side. In a works break, look at a side of the post and hold F for half a second. The pioneer sergeant puts the next piece the rule allows on that side, straight away. There are eight sides, picked by where you’re looking. The first hold wins, and everyone else gets a toast with the name. If nobody holds for 12 seconds, the rule’s piece goes up as before. You can’t shut a gate, because the squad and the lorries need the way through. If you look into one it’s refused, and the prompt tells you to look out past it.
  • WIRE THE GATE. From wave 6 the pioneers can string concertina across a gateway. The gap stays open, but infantry in it move at less than half speed. A trooper spends a second longer in the coils under the gate’s gun, and a rifleman three. Your own men and the lorries go through at full speed. One per gate.
  • A break fits in 19 seconds now. The column under the wave count shows what the next wave asks of you, one line per thing. Intelligence gets three lines. Recon, the works and the dispatch get one each. In a section of four or more the list is name and class only, and RP is a column on the Tab roll call. The pay line shows on a run’s first break and whenever the pay changes. Teaching lines stay for a career’s first three breaks, then move to the field manual. A six-man break went from 24 lines to 16, and at HUD 130% it doesn’t run into the squad list any more.
  • The ace is a boss. In the final assault the enemy ace comes last, and he’s named in the break before. Until he’s crippled, only his weak points count: the deck, the sides and the running gear. A mine under his belly throws a track. Once he’s crippled, everything counts. His bounty is 50% more, and killing him with the post above half pins GIANT KILLER. Solo, the AT crate’s five rockets kill a Panzer ace with one to spare and a Tiger ace with none. The gun’s AP and satchels are your margin.
  • THE ARMOURED TRAIN. On Factory District’s rail yard, the last act of the final assault is a Panzerzug. It steams in on the far track, stops broadside 60 m out and has to be fought car by car. Put rifles on the flak crew at the nose and rockets on the turret and the boiler. The plate takes nothing until the boiler’s blown. Then hit the magazine car for the kill. Three times it puts a company off onto the field. The relief column comes up past the wreck. Solo it takes six of the crate’s eleven rockets. With six players it’s fourteen of thirty-six.
  • THE ROAD IS CUT. In HOLD THE LINE on a German front, Hauptmann Hartkopf, “Der Riegel”, turns up when the lorries are called. He brings a King Tiger down the road with the surge and halts past the evacuation point. The lorries won’t set off while he’s standing, and the clock reads ROAD CUT. Throw his track (two rockets, or one satchel) and they come at once. CLEAR ROAD pays if you open it inside their minute. If he’s still standing at 90 seconds, the road stays cut and the line goes on. On the Japanese fronts it’s Taisa Hayashida’s charge instead: the Colonel on horseback with his colours, four sabres, a Chi-Ha and a banzai behind them. Shoot the horse and he’s on foot by the road with his sword out, sending in every man within 30 m every 14 seconds. Cut him down, or cripple the Chi-Ha, and the road opens.
  • You get into the lorries yourself. The lorries drive in, swing round and park facing out, tailgates to the post. F at a tailgate seats you with your rifle live. Four fit in a bed, and one man can take the ring-mounted .30 cal on the lead cab (F at the cab step). They leave 30 seconds after they arrive, or 5 seconds after the last living soldier and his squad are aboard. A man down within 3 m of a tailgate gets hauled in. Anyone not aboard is LEFT ON THE LINE. He gets no evacuation pay and no decoration, and his name goes on everyone’s telegram. Those aboard ride out firing for 12 seconds. The squad climbs in behind the first of you.
  • The MG nest counter-attack is gone. Shooting the crew on the way in paid as a win, so rather than tune it we took it out. The mortar pit, the dispatches, the crossroads and each front’s own job are still there, and they start at wave 3 now.
  • Four idle men at the post. The four men standing round a tin at the post were HQ’s brew-up scenery. A deployment bug was putting them into the field on co-op clients. That’s fixed. While we were in there we fixed a few other things. Followers don’t stand in a knot any more. A man kneeling with an enemy in plain view now fires. A client sees every one of the host’s squad shots.
  • Smaller things. The AT crate now warns “you’ll leave the flamethrower”, and leaves it at your feet so you can take it back. DONATE repeats while held, and DONATE ALL asks once. The field glasses say GUNS SILENT, FIRE MISSION INBOUND or the kills you’re short, and never show a full meter beside NOT READY. The Grease Gun’s Flash Hider is a Basic.

The BRIEFING answers four questions

Changed
  • The left column is four fields, always in the same place: WHERE · NEXT DEPLOYMENT, WHICH RUN, HOW HARD · WHAT IT PAYS, and YOUR KIT. Each has a caption, the answer in a line or two, and a button to change it. Your kit sits at the same height whatever your rank.
  • WHERE names the front and says whether it’s SECURED, how many waves win it and your best. It also gives the first line of the front’s story. CHANGE FRONT is on the right, where it always was.
  • The board is off the page: the A / B / C rows, OP, OR with its arrows and “1/7”, CMP and the warning lines. One field now says which run you’re on, and CHANGE RUN opens the rest.
  • “ORDERS FROM HQ” is gone from the dispatches. The board’s good surprises are now called a GIFT instead of an anomaly.
  • Nothing on the page is smaller than 14 pt any more. If a line would have shrunk to fit, it’s cut short instead.

Which run: one field and one sheet

New
  • The WHICH RUN field has three lines. The first is the run’s letter and a plain title (C · THE HARDEST RUN, WEEKLY OPERATION, DAILY ORDERS, CHALLENGE, THE CAMPAIGN). The second is its modifiers, a pip and a name each. The third says what the pips mean. Until you’ve taken a dealt run, it says “two harder runs pay more: CHANGE RUN” instead.
  • CHANGE RUN opens the RUN sheet. TODAY’S BOARD is three cards side by side. Each shows the letter, the title, what it pays, and a line per modifier with its effect. The one you’re on says SELECTED. Underneath, OTHER WAYS TO PLAY lists the weekly operation, the daily orders, the campaign and each challenge as rows you can take.
  • THE MAP block shows the next deployment’s conditions the same way, a pip and a name each, in ink. It used to be names joined with a plus sign, in red.
  • In a session, a client’s field shows the host’s pick and says “the host picks”. SEE THE BOARD lets you read it.

One figure for what a run pays

Changed
  • HOW HARD · WHAT IT PAYS gives one total in red (“pays RP x3.00 · XP x1.90”), with the parts under it in whole percents: “Standard tier · 2 handicaps +55% · Pact heat 9 +72% · run C +44%”. The same total is on the deploy bar, at the foot of the difficulty sheet and in the TEAM header. It’s exact. It’s what your run pays at wave 1.
  • The difficulty sheet’s tabs now read TIER / HANDICAPS / THE PACT. Every figure on them is a part of the total (“+55% RP”, “pays as is”, “heat 9 · +72% RP”). When the cap bites, the sheet tells you, and says what it caps.
  • The field shows whenever there’s a difficulty to speak of. The CHANGE button only shows when there’s a dial to set. Until you’ve secured a front, you change the run’s pay with CHANGE RUN.
  • A new soldier sees a Replacements line instead of a difficulty. A client sees the host’s pick.

Cards you can read in two seconds

Changed
  • One line, nine words at most. All 171 cards (commendations, veteran cards and the Quartermaster’s Issue) have a hand-written face line. The trigger goes first and the number last: “Headshot kill -> a round back into the gun.” The old paragraph is still there, one hold away.
  • Marks instead of sentences. The school is a lettered badge on the card’s foot (M, S, CQ, D, F, SG, +) with pips. Ink pips are the cards you hold of that school. The brass pip is this card. A brass ring means it brings a set rule in. Beside the name you might see a gold seal for a rule-breaker, an arrow box for a transform, II for a Rank II copy, stack bars, gold pair brackets that close when you hold the partner, a struck badge for REPLACES, or a red diamond for a cursed crate. Gun cards carry the gun in a blue box. Class cards have a square with the class’s initial.
  • There’s at most one brass tag over the top edge: COMPLETES a pair, SET: a rule it brings in, or FROM a mate.
  • Bigger type. The name is 22 pt and the face 18. Nothing on a card is under 16 except the gun’s box, and nothing shrinks to fit any more.
  • A Rank II copy’s face only says what the second rank adds. We re-mixed the seven school colours so colour-blind players can tell them all apart. The letters tell you anyway.
  • We shortened five long names: Q.M.’s Favourite, Regimental Colours, Fresh Riflemen, Loose Rounds, Mule-Borne Ammo, and Hand-Loaded Ammo. They’re the same cards, with the same ids.

Tap Y for the whole rule

New
  • Tap Y on the cards and every card opens to its full rule, with a line per mark explaining it (“SET  Close Quarters 3/3 -> COLD STEEL: …”). They stay open until you tap again or pick. If you hold it for 0.8 s instead, they only stay open while you hold it. (The first build was hold only. The recut added the tap.) You can pick at any time, open or not.
  • We rewrote 28 of the full rules to put in the numbers the old text left out, like Belt Swap’s 8 s, Covering Fire’s 1 s linger and Shock Rounds’ odds. Every safety clause and cooldown is in the hold.
  • The first time you get an offer, a one-time caption says “Tap [Y]: the full rule”.

The strip

Changed
  • The card strip follows HUD SIZE now, not MENU TEXT. It keeps clear of the compass, the weapon corner and the crosshair. Five or six cards come in two rows at full size instead of one squeezed row.
  • The header is one line (“FIELD COMMENDATION · 1-3”, or “[Y] + 1-3” in a fight). The reroll and chit lines are short, and there’s a PITY +20% tag. The “No hurry: it waits into the next wave” line only shows on your first three offers.
  • In a break the world dims behind the strip, and the number keys work as soon as the break starts.
  • Mates’ picks show under the kill feed. You’ll see “DAN took HOT STREAK II” with the school badge for 3 s, on every machine. It replaces the host’s old “X took LEGENDARY” chat alert. Completed pairs and section sets still post their gold chat alerts.

The codex, the dossier and the Tab sheet

Changed
  • RECORDS › CODEX shows every card’s face in the grid, and the full card on the right with its source, state and odds. After this update, DRAW RULES opens once on THE MARKS, a key to every symbol.
  • The commendation dossier at HQ shows the real cards. Rest the mouse on one for its full rule.
  • The Tab sheet shows mini cards with the badge. The ringed card’s box shows its face, then the rule. Set rows show the badge, the pips and the rule’s name.
  • RULES IN FORCE leads each tag with its badge, seal or pair mark.

The works build themselves

Changed
  • Recut: this is the fallback now. In a works break you can look at a side and hold F to put the piece there yourself. What follows is what happens when nobody does. See The day after.
  • No vote. From rank 8 the section still earns pioneers from waves held: one a wave, and one more per 15 kills. Every third break the pioneer sergeant puts up the piece the ground asks for, facing where recon says the next wave is coming from. It stands for the whole deployment.
  • The rule is printed, not voted. It’s a listening post before wave 5 if the pioneers can pay for it. Otherwise it’s wire, then a breastwork, then a pit, then the parapet: the first one that side hasn’t got and the pioneers can pay for. If nothing can be sited or paid for, the pioneers are kept for next time.
  • NEXT BREAK names it a break ahead (“THE WORKS – wire, west”), and the banner says what went up. The works panel is one line in a quiet break and stays off the screen while a question is up. The works don’t ask you anything, so they can go up in a question break too. Only the march keeps them off.
  • LANE INTEL and WORKS on RULES IN FORCE are as they were. K does nothing.

One question a break

New
  • Hold Tab, tap 1 or 2. Push on, a section dare, the route, the campaign’s fork, the lorries and HOLD THE LINE? all ask the same way now. 1 is the first answer (push on, take the dare, route A, call the lorries). 2 is the second (route B). Tap your answer again to take it back. Let go of Tab and nothing changes.
  • One panel, where the ready panel was. It shows the question, its two answers with their terms, each soldier’s answer as it comes in, and the clock. In brass it says what happens if nobody answers (“Nobody: the break as usual, mend and make ready”). It’s always fine to leave it.
  • One question a break, never two. A route or fork vote is 12 s now, down from 20, and it closes 3 s after everyone has answered. Push on is 8 s, a dare 10 s and the lorries 20 s.
  • E only readies you (and, held, repairs the post if you’re the engineer). Holding E to push on, as in playtest 20, is gone. Solo, Tab + 1 pushes on, and a tap of E declines it and starts the wave.
  • With a card offer up, Tab + 1 answers the question and picks no card. 1 on its own picks a card and answers nothing.

Together

Co-op
  • The TEAM header now shows the run’s total (“HOSTING · pays RP x3.00”) instead of the Pact heat, so it matches the BRIEFING and the bar. If the run pays as is, nothing’s added.
  • The host’s line says what the host decides: the front, the run and the difficulty for everyone. DEPLOY never waits for ready ticks. Roster names fit before they shrink, and your own folds “(you)” to “·you” when it has to.
  • A client’s BRIEFING fields show the host’s choices, with “the host picks” where the buttons would be.
  • The field manual’s first lobby note now says: pick a gun and a specialist under LOADOUT, then MARK READY. The host picks the run.

Keys

Changed
  • Tap Y on a card offer for the full rule and a line per mark. Tap again to close. U rerolls where you can.
  • J, K and L do nothing now. None of them was in reach while your hands were busy moving and aiming.
  • Give a find you’re looking at to a mate with X in a wave. X is also the rifle grenade’s key. A press that gives doesn’t load the launcher, and every other press does. (The recut took out the card hand-off that also used this key.)
  • Your field kit is on Z, next to your movement hand. Ping is on Q alone now. Z used to be its second key.
  • Hold F looking at a side in a works break to put the works there. Hold it at a lorry’s tailgate to get aboard, or at the lead cab’s step to man its .30 cal.
  • Hold Tab and tap 1 or 2 to answer the section’s question. A tap of E readies you. Hold-E to push on, from playtest 20, is gone. A medic holding E in a break plants the Aid Post. An engineer holding it repairs, as before.
  • You can rebind all of them at OPTIONS › CONTROLS, which has one row fewer now.

Your save

Changed
  • Your save is untouched. Every card, kit, boon and level loads as it was. The only cards that behave differently are the ammo cards and Chain Reaction, as above.
  • A key you rebound yourself is kept. If your own ping binding kept Z, your field kit starts with no key rather than sharing it. The WHAT’S NEW card tells you once, and you can set one in OPTIONS › CONTROLS.
  • A soldier who’s already had offers never sees the “it waits” line. A new soldier sees it three times. The codex’s THE MARKS page opens itself once.
  • Everyone in a co-op game needs playtest 21. The mates’ picks line goes over the network, so an older build gets a plain message to update instead of silently dropping out.

Fixes

Fixed
  • The recut: a RECON panel from a run you’d left in a break sat over the range course’s station card. It would also have named a false main thrust on your next first wave. Recon’s bearings are forgotten when a run ends now, and the wave column never draws on the range.
  • The recut: the range’s marker sat on the target’s chest with its distance under it, which hid a 50 m target. It sits above the head now and names a place to get to, with the distance (“THE PEG – 21 M”). The objective is a diamond on the compass too.
  • The recut: on a client’s after-action card, soldiers who had left got paired up with the wrong rows, so two soldiers showed twice with each other’s kills and accolades. Each soldier is matched by his machine now, and a soldier who leaves keeps his own figures.
  • The recut: the M1903A4’s Lyman peep ring and front blade took on the rifle’s camo, so a snow-white sight vanished against night fog and looked inaccurate. They stay dark steel now. The aim itself was fine.
  • The BRIEFING, the bar and the TEAM header now all show the same pay. Before, a host on run C could read “pays RP x1.78” on the bar while the page showed nothing.
  • At HUD 130% the three longest held cards lost their last line. Now a mark line gives way first. At MENU TEXT 130% the codex and dossier grew each wrapped line on its own. They don’t any more.
  • A number no longer breaks from its unit at a line end (“8 / m”).
  • On 16:9, three or more cards at HUD 130% can’t fit between the HUD’s corners, so the strip steps down to 115% or 100% rather than narrowing a card. As a last resort the squad roster and the STEADYING label fold away under it.
  • Reduce motion (OPTIONS › weapon sway and bob off) now also stills the Legendary gleam.
  • The first-time “two harder runs pay more” hint only shows while run A is picked. Once you’ve found the sheet, it shows the legend instead.

Known issues

  • Holding Y on a two-row strip in a fight covers the crosshair while it’s held.
  • On 4:3 and 16:10 screens with HUD 130% and five or six cards, the strip may touch the compass or the Spirit meter. On a 4:3 screen the dossier cuts off long names.
  • The glyph letters on the school badges are small at 720p. Tell us if you can’t read one.
  • We set the BRIEFING’s layout, the pay field’s rule and the works’ cadence of three for this playtest. They may change after it.
  • The recut’s changes were checked by machines and against the first session’s reports. Brenton played it once on the evening of the 28th, which is where the four fixes above came from. Nobody else has. Try the boss fights and the lorries first. Their timings come from scripted shots, not from people.

What we’d like you to test

  • Open a card offer and pick without holding Y. Did the face line and the marks tell you enough? Which card made you hold?
  • Did the marks make sense before you read THE MARKS in the codex? Which one didn’t?
  • On the BRIEFING, could you tell where you were going, which run you were on and what it paid without opening anything? Did the one pay figure match what you got?
  • Did you find CHANGE RUN, and did the RUN sheet’s cards read better than the old rows?
  • The section’s question: did you find Tab + 1 without being told? Did you ever pick a card when you meant to answer, or answer when you meant to pick? Did the brass “Nobody:” line make it fine to leave it?
  • The works: did you notice them go up, and did NEXT BREAK’s line tell you where to look? Does the base feel like it builds itself, or barely builds at all?
  • The recut: with the column trimmed, could you read the fight? Did you miss the section roster in a fight, or find it on Tab? Is 19 s enough with the cards? Did a tap of Y in a fight ever get in your way?
  • Medics: did the Aid Post go down in the break on a hold of E, and did a tap still ready you?
  • Overnight: did the Quartermaster’s item make sense for what the section needed, and did you miss picking it? Did the offers still feel like ammo, ammo, ammo? Go out past the wire and shoot a squad: did they come for you, and did the post hold while they did?
  • The day after: did you find hold F for the works without being told, and did the piece go where you meant? Did the wired gate feel closed enough? Was the ace a fight or a chore, and could you tell where to hit him? In HOLD THE LINE, did everyone get aboard, and did the man left on the line feel fairly treated?
  • Did X ever load your rifle grenade when you meant to give a find, or the other way round? Is the field kit easier to reach on Z?
  • In a section, did the mates’ picks line tell you anything useful? Did the TEAM header’s pay match the host’s BRIEFING?
  • Did anything on a card, the strip or the BRIEFING get cut off or overlap at your resolution and HUD size? Say which.
  • Press F10 the moment something looks wrong. It sends the map code for you.
