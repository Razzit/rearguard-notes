Named aces lead the final assault

New

The last tank on every front now has a commander with a name and a reputation, rolled each run. A title card tells you who’s coming and what he means to do. His kills are ringed on his barrel and his mark is on his turret.

Panzer IV · 31 kills

Hauptmann Ritter, “Schwarze Sieben”

Shells you from behind a smoke screen, then rolls in with men riding his hull. They hang on and don’t shoot, so they are rifle targets until they jump down.

24 kills

Oberleutnant Reineke, “Der Fuchs”

Works round your flank with a half-track full of men behind him, then breaks in close.

47 kills

Major Adler, “Der Alte”

Backs away from anyone who closes in, front plate always to you, then calls mortars down on the nearest soldier. Four stonks, then he has to come to you.

Henderson Field · Chi-Nu

Taii Kageyama, “Kurogane”

His riders leap off in a banzai charge with the bugle, and he follows them in.

  • Cripple him. Rockets, a satchel or enough grenades at the tracks and a track comes off: he’s stuck where he is, the turret still firing. Finish him at the engine deck.
  • Through the letterbox. Put a round through his vision slit and the crew are stunned for a moment. It plays the headshot dink. The Tiger and the Chi-Nu have a slit now too.
  • Whoever lands the finishing blow is named on the kill card, and on the telegram home. The tank’s armour, gun and pay are unchanged: only his plan is new.

Jerry’s Fire Plan

New

The enemy has a few tricks up his sleeve, and intelligence won’t hear about them. Two or three times a deployment, from wave 3, something big happens in the middle of a wave.

The Doodlebug

Listen for the buzz of a flying bomb crossing the sky. When the engine cuts out you have four seconds: TAKE COVER, get down. It leaves a crater for the rest of the deployment.

Rolling Barrage

A wall of shells walks right over the post with men behind it. Get inside the command post’s room or under a roof, then STAND TO when it lifts: they’re right behind it.

From wave 5

Goliath Swarm

Little tracked bombs crawl at the sandbags on long wires. Shoot them in the field and they only hurt the enemy; let one reach the wall and it breaches it. Or follow the wires back to the man kneeling at his controls. Drop him and every one of them stops dead.

The Paymaster

A staff car races across the far field for twenty seconds. Get the officer in the back and his satchel earns every man in the section an extra commendation at the next break.

Every front fights its own war

Changed
  • Each front has its own habits: cavalry in the Normandy lanes, paratroopers over the Ardennes, snipers on Monte Lungo’s terraces, their guns duelling ours in the Factory District, infiltrators and banzai rushes on Henderson Field.
  • New counter-attacks on real ground: blow the ammunition stacked by the cider press in the farm’s barn, fire the fuel dump in the Ardennes pines (it lights the trees at night), jam the sewer grate men keep climbing out of in the Factory District, clear a German observer out of Monte Lungo’s bell tower before his battery finds you, and walk a downed pilot back in on Henderson Field.
  • The first waves don’t open the same way twice. You might get a recon patrol, a mortar ranging on the wire, a sniper, horsemen in the lanes or a lorry of shock troops at the gate.
  • Half-way through every front comes a set piece: a dawn bayonet rush from three sides on the farm, a sewer breakout inside the wire in the Factory, the whole battery on Monte Lungo’s piazza, a banzai wave behind a Ha-Go on Henderson. In the Ardennes, when word comes that some of our men are making their way back to the post, look hard at their helmets.
  • Same pay, same numbers: it’s the same men, coming a different way.

Veteran formations

New enemies

From wave 4, any ordinary trooper can wear a coloured armband. Aim at him and his mark shows. Seven marks, and each one changes who you shoot first.

Pioneer

Lays a mine where he dies. Shoot it and it goes off as your blast, hurting only his mates. Step near it and it jumps.

Fanatic

Gets up once at 1 HP. A headshot keeps him down.

Storm Trooper

Throws smoke the moment he spots the post.

Grenadier

Cooks his stick grenades. The throwback window is tight.

Sergeant

Chevrons. Every man within 8 m of him shoots straighter while he lives. Kill him first.

Medic

Red cross. Revives one fallen man once, unless you interrupt him. A man shot through the head can’t be got up.

Runner

Faster, and goes to ground less.

At most two veterans alive at once, plus one for every two soldiers in the section. They’re worth a little more, the kill feed names them, and a VETERAN MARKS card opens under ENEMY FILES once you’ve met one.

Spirit, grades and the After-Action Report

New
  • Your Spirit climbs from DUTIFUL through STEADY, KEEN and GALLANT to V FOR VICTORY in the corner by your weapon. Mix your blows: a headshot, then the bayonet, then a grenade. The same trick over and over wears thin, and standing idle lets it slip. It multiplies a running score for you and the section. It is recognition only and never changes damage or requisition.
  • Field citations name your best tricks and pay five times over the first time each wave: Through the Letterbox, Return to Sender, Over the Top, Two Birds, Coup de Grace, Long Shot, Point Blank and Cold Steel.
  • Every wave held is stamped D to S, or V for Victory, on the WAVE HELD banner. Keep the post whole, clear them out smartly and keep your spirits up.
  • The After-Action Report on the results screen: your overall grade stamped on an Army Form, every wave’s grade, and a superlative for every man in the section, from Crack Shot of the Day to Tea Boy and Bullet Magnet. Your best grade on each front goes on your Service Record, and the roll call (Tab) shows everyone’s Spirit.

The Last Round

New
  • Every wave now ends on a newsreel. The shot that drops the wave’s last man plays again on everyone’s screen in slow motion: from the muzzle, along the tracer, to the man. Bazooka rounds, grenades, satchels and fire missions get the same treatment, and a Panzer that goes up last goes up again.
  • The caption names who did it, with what, and from how far: “PVT. ELLIS – KAR98K – 212 YDS”. A headshot gets the old dink and THROUGH THE HELMET. Finish a wave inside a Crack Shot and it’s billed as one.
  • It lasts about a second. Any key skips it, and your commendations wait until it’s done. Don’t want it? OPTIONS › Last round camera. In a section, the host’s setting holds.

The Sar’nt-Major is on the net

New

A parade-ground voice over a crackling field radio now calls the moments.

  • Your own: HEADSHOT! on a run of heads, HAT-TRICK! and FOUR OF A KIND! on a quick string of kills, CRACK SHOT!, and COUP DE GRACE! when a grenade you threw back drops its owner.
  • The section’s: STAND TO! with the wave’s number, WAVE HELD – WELL DONE, THE REARGUARD!, LAST MAN STANDING – GOOD LUCK, LAD!, and FRONT SECURED! He calls out a Tiger on the road, a Stuka diving and a breached post.
  • Your mates: their big streaks are called with their callsign (“ELLIS – HAT-TRICK!”). With six of you, he still calls only one every ten seconds, and he never talks over himself or HQ.
  • OPTIONS › AUDIO: turn him off, set his level, or pick a British or American voice. With radio subtitles on, his calls are written at the foot of the screen too.

Anti-tank gun

New

Every front now has a 6-pounder dug in inside the wire (a 37 mm on Henderson Field, a captured Pak 40 in the Factory District). Its shells come up with the first AT crate.

  • F at the gun takes a seat. The gunner lays and fires it; R swaps armour-piercing and high-explosive. Push past the stops and the whole trail heaves round.
  • A second soldier on the loader’s seat loads it in 1.5 s instead of 4. On your own, a squadmate comes to load for you.
  • AP hits like a bazooka and the weak sides still count; HE clears a knot of infantry and never hurts your own.
  • Tanks will turn on a manned gun first. Two shells knock it out: hold F at it to dig it out. Brew up armour with two on the gun and you both get the new GUN CREW mention.

New cards
Quartermaster’s Issue · 9 new cards

Field Telephone

Ring up and ask for trouble. Snipers, flamethrowers or a Stuka come next wave, and if you hold it everyone’s next commendation is a tier better. Once a deployment.

Section cards

Captured MG42s, Mine Belt, Sniper Team, Wire Party

A captured MG42 on a post tripod, a belt of friendly-safe mines across the ways in, two GIs on the roof for three waves, a new belt of wire. Section cards help everyone once; doubling up just tops up your pouches.

A friendly patrol

The Marines, the Resistance, the Airborne, Italian partisans or Commandos work the flank for a wave, and fewer riflemen arrive.

Your own

Smoke Grenades, Medic’s Bag, Morale Ration

Your grenades lay smoke for three waves, revives go faster and lift a man to 75%, or your health comes back sooner.

Legendary pair

The flamethrower gets its Legendaries. Backdraft and Bottomless Tank combine into a chain of fireballs that keeps the tank full.

Up the Rearguard!

New
  • Press N in a fight to raise your helmet on your muzzle and cheer. Every soldier who joins in within five seconds adds +5% reload speed for the whole section for ten seconds, up to +30% with six. One rally a wave, so make it count, and if everyone cheers you all earn ALL TOGETHER.
  • Brew up. In a wave break the same key raises a mug of tea instead. It does nothing at all, and that’s the point.
  • Re-enlisting means something now. Each Service Star puts a service chevron by your name and pays a re-enlistment bounty: +10% RP for your next 20 waves. A souvenir MP40 waits for you at the command post (Star I) and a scoped Kar98k joins it (Star III). Veterans’ orders at Star II (Head Hunters, Walking Wounded, Worn Kit) for those who want it harder and better paid, ten new Veteran Traits, and a gold frame round your name at Star V.

Bring your own men

Changed
  • In co-op every soldier now brings one of his own hired riflemen, not just the host. Your hires and your Platoon tree count in a friend’s game: his health, aim, issued weapon and field promotions are yours.
  • The section fields up to 3 men for 1 to 3 soldiers, 4 for 4 or 5, and 5 for a full section of six. The host’s first man goes first, then one for each friend in the order you joined.
  • Your man wears your name: YOURS on the roster, “ELLIS’S MAN” under a friend’s crosshair. When you go down, he comes for you first. SQUAD STAYS BEHIND now works for everyone.
  • Big sections: past three soldiers the waves come denser, not longer. More men at once, and more paratroopers, snipers, half-track infantry and cavalry. One to three soldiers play exactly as before.

Hold the line has a way out

Changed
  • CALL THE LORRIES. After every fifth wave past the relief, the section can vote to call the lorries. Hold the far gate for 90 seconds against everything they’ve got; at the minute the lorries come up the track with their lamps lit.
  • Get aboard before they pull out, or carry a wounded mate to the tailgate (hold F on him and walk). Get away and the run ends Evacuated with Honour: your pay on the line goes up from 60% to 90%, and there’s a new decoration in it. Overrun on the way out? It’s an ordinary overrun. You keep everything; only the bonus is lost.
  • Ten new threats on the line, introduced one at a time with a card: Wire Cutters, Sturm Company, Officer’s Staff, Sniper Screen, Rocket Mortars, Ghost Company, Flak Barrage (get under a roof!), King Tiger and Kampfgruppe. On Henderson Field, a Banzai Company and a Tank Company. At wave ten past the relief, night falls.
  • Every fifth wave the radio has a job for you: hold the crossroads, silence the mortar pit, or bring back the dispatches. BRIEFING shows your best evacuation on each front.

Every map checked, every spawn fair

Fixed
  • Before you deploy, BRIEFING shows the map you’ll fight on: its code, its name, what sets it apart, today’s conditions, and a small sketch of the ground. In a section, everyone sees the host’s map before pressing READY.
  • We built every front on 20,000 map codes and checked every spawn, hiding place, mortar pit, MG nest, gate and vault on each one.
  • Monte Lungo: mortar teams now set up down the terraces where the wall can see them, not hidden at a spot nobody could reach. A mortar team hidden behind a hedge or in the jungle no longer counts as “in sight”.
  • Nobody spawns inside a bush’s leaves, a cover box or a burnt-out truck, and burnt trucks sit in the same place for every player. Your machine guns get a clear lane of fire: no more pine tree in front of the Ardennes gun. An enemy who gets stuck on scenery is freed within seconds.
  • A few maps you kept from playtest 17 may look slightly different close to the camp.

Readable at a glance, bright by default

Changed
  • Night is no longer pitch black. Night Attack, Full Moon and Last Light keep their mood, but you can see the men at the wire. The enemy sees no better than before. Flares burn longer and brighter.
  • New OPTIONS › VIDEO page: Brightness, “Night: keep it bright” (on by default) and Render scale. Aim down sights can be HOLD or TOGGLE. Menu text can be set to 115% or 130%. In a friend’s game you can skip the host’s Crack Shot slow motion on your own screen.
  • The HUD has a place for everything. Banners no longer stack on the wave title, the combo sits just above the crosshair, and nothing runs into anything at 720p or at 130% HUD size. Blitz, Molten Barrel, Pinpoint and friends show a small timer over your weapon list.
  • The BRIEFING tells you when the new conditions arrive in your own time, and the board never changes in the middle of a deployment.

Report from the field

New
  • Something broke, looked wrong or felt unfair? Press F10 (or REPORT A PROBLEM in the pause menu, or REPORT at HQ). Say what happened in a line or two and press SEND. It goes straight to #feedback in the RearGuard Discord.
  • A picture of the screen from the moment you pressed it goes too, with the front, wave, map code, conditions and Honours. No more copying “Map 4F2A” down by hand. Untick the picture if you’d rather not send it. Tick “Something I’d change” to send a suggestion instead.
  • It never sends your chat or anyone’s address. No connection? The report is kept and offered again from the pause menu. One report a minute.
  • The War Diary. RECORDS › WAR DIARY now writes up every deployment, a line a day: where you held, how long, and who was with you.

Fixes

Fixed
  • One player’s game crashing no longer drops the whole section. Before, a client going down hard took every other client with it and the host then refused new joins. Only the one who crashed leaves now, and a fresh player can join.
  • Your service record is now backed up. The game keeps the last few saves beside it and writes each new one safely, so a crash or power cut mid-save can no longer wipe your progress. If a save is ever damaged, the most recent good backup loads and the Briefing tells you; the damaged file is kept, never written over.
  • Old builds can’t join a new host any more: you get a plain message to update instead of a silent drop. The post is full at six.
  • Join mid-run and you now see the mass assault, the runners and any Stuka already in the air, and you get a commendation offer for each wave already held (up to five).
  • The stutter at the start of a wave (about a second, the first time cavalry rode in) is gone, and so are the shorter stutters the first time you heard a flamethrower, a rocket battery or a Stuka. Joining players no longer freeze when the first horses appear.
  • Half-track squads and tank escorts get out beside their vehicle instead of being pushed away from it.
  • Developer switches no longer work in the release game.

What we’d like you to test

  • Nothing in this build has been played by a person yet. Press F10 the moment something looks wrong; it sends the map code for you.
  • The Sar’nt-Major: too chatty, too quiet, or about right? Which voice?
  • The Last Round cam: does it earn its second every wave, or do you start skipping it?
  • The aces: can you read each one’s plan? Is Adler too passive once his mortars run out, and are the riders a good target or a cheap one?
  • Fire Plan: did the Doodlebug’s silence land? Were the Goliaths a puzzle or a nuisance in a full section?
  • Spirit and grades: does a rifle player who only takes heads feel short-changed?
  • The anti-tank gun: does a tank turning on your gun feel thrilling or punishing? Is the gun decisive enough from the front?
  • Night: too bright now? You can turn “keep it bright” off in VIDEO and tell us which you prefer.
  • Hold the line: would you call the lorries at five, or push on? Did anyone get carried aboard?
  • Bring your own men: did your friend’s man show up with the right kit, and did he come for you when you went down?
