There’s a lot in this one. Every wave now ends on a short newsreel of the last kill. The last tank on each front is a named ace with a plan of his own. Jerry has some new tricks, like a flying bomb that cuts its engine right over the post. A Sar’nt-Major calls your headshots on the radio. Each front fights its own war now, and there’s a crewed anti-tank gun inside the wire. In co-op, everyone brings one of their own men. And the two bugs that could drop the whole section or wipe a save are fixed.

Named aces lead the final assault

New

The last tank on every front now has a named commander with a reputation, picked at random each run. A title card tells you who’s coming and what he’s going to try. His kills are painted as rings on the barrel, and his mark is on the turret.

Panzer IV · 31 kills

Hauptmann Ritter, “Schwarze Sieben”

Shells you from behind a smoke screen, then rolls in with men riding on the hull. They hang on and don’t shoot, so you can pick them off with a rifle until they jump down.

24 kills

Oberleutnant Reineke, “Der Fuchs”

Works round your flank with a half-track full of men behind him, then comes in close.

47 kills

Major Adler, “Der Alte”

Backs off from anyone who gets close and keeps his front plate toward you. Then he calls mortars down on the nearest soldier. He has four stonks. After that he has to come to you.

Henderson Field · Chi-Nu

Taii Kageyama, “Kurogane”

His riders jump off when the bugle goes and make a banzai charge. He follows them in.

  • Cripple him. Hit the tracks with rockets, a satchel or enough grenades and a track comes off. He can’t move, but the turret keeps firing. Finish him at the engine deck.
  • Through the letterbox. A round through his vision slit stuns the crew for a moment, and you’ll hear the headshot dink. The Tiger and the Chi-Nu have a slit now too.
  • Whoever lands the finishing blow is named on the kill card and on the telegram home. The tank itself hasn’t changed. Its armour, gun and pay are the same as before. Only the plan is new.

Jerry’s Fire Plan

New

Jerry has a few new tricks, and intelligence won’t warn you about them. From wave 3, two or three times a deployment, something big happens in the middle of a wave.

The Doodlebug

Listen for the buzz of a flying bomb crossing the sky. When the engine cuts out you’ve got four seconds. TAKE COVER and get down. It leaves a crater that stays for the rest of the deployment.

Rolling Barrage

A wall of shells walks right over the post, with men following it in. Get inside the post’s room or under a roof. When it lifts, STAND TO. They’re right behind it.

From wave 5

Goliath Swarm

Little tracked bombs on long wires crawl toward the sandbags. Shoot one out in the field and it only hurts the enemy. Let one reach the wall and it breaches it. You can also follow the wires back to the man kneeling at the controls. Kill him and they all stop.

The Paymaster

A staff car races across the far field for 20 seconds. Shoot the officer in the back and his satchel gets every man in the section an extra commendation at the next break.

Every front fights its own war

Changed
  • Each front has its own habits. There’s cavalry in the Normandy lanes and paratroopers over the Ardennes. Monte Lungo has snipers on the terraces. In the Factory District their guns duel ours, and Henderson Field gets infiltrators and banzai rushes.
  • New counter-attacks, on real places in each map. On the farm, there’s ammunition stacked by the cider press in the barn for you to blow. In the Ardennes pines you set fire to a fuel dump, which lights up the trees at night. Men keep climbing out of a sewer grate in the Factory District until you jam it. On Monte Lungo, get the German observer out of the bell tower before his battery finds you. On Henderson Field you walk a downed pilot back in.
  • The first waves don’t open the same way twice. You might get a recon patrol, a mortar ranging on the wire, a sniper, horsemen in the lanes or a lorry of shock troops at the gate.
  • Every front has a set piece half-way through. On the farm it’s a dawn bayonet rush from three sides. In the Factory they break out of the sewers inside the wire. Monte Lungo gets the whole battery on the piazza, and Henderson a banzai wave behind a Ha-Go. In the Ardennes, when word comes that some of our men are making their way back to the post, look hard at their helmets.
  • The pay and the numbers haven’t changed. It’s the same enemy coming at you a different way.

Veteran formations

New enemies

From wave 4, any ordinary trooper might be wearing a coloured armband. Aim at him and you’ll see his mark. There are seven, and each one changes who you should shoot first.

Pioneer

Leaves a mine where he dies. Shoot it and it counts as your blast, so it only hurts his mates. Step near it and it jumps.

Fanatic

Gets up once at 1 HP. A headshot keeps him down.

Storm Trooper

Throws smoke the moment he spots the post.

Grenadier

Cooks his stick grenades. The throwback window is tight.

Sergeant

Chevrons. Every man within 8 m of him shoots straighter while he lives. Kill him first.

Medic

Red cross. He can revive one fallen man, once, unless you interrupt him. A man shot through the head can’t be got up.

Runner

Faster, and goes to ground less.

There are never more than two veterans alive at once, plus one more for every two soldiers in the section. They pay a little more and the kill feed names them. Once you’ve met one, a VETERAN MARKS card opens under ENEMY FILES.

Spirit, grades and the After-Action Report

New
  • Your Spirit sits in the corner by your weapon. It climbs from DUTIFUL through STEADY, KEEN and GALLANT to V FOR VICTORY. To raise it, mix things up: a headshot, then the bayonet, then a grenade. Doing the same thing over and over wears thin, and standing about lets it slip. It multiplies a running score for you and the section. It’s only there for recognition. It never changes damage or requisition.
  • Field citations name your best tricks. Each pays five times over the first time you pull it off in a wave. They are Through the Letterbox, Return to Sender, Over the Top, Two Birds, Coup de Grace, Long Shot, Point Blank and Cold Steel.
  • Every wave you hold gets a grade from D to S, or V for Victory, stamped on the WAVE HELD banner. To grade well, keep the post in one piece, clear them out smartly and keep your Spirit up.
  • The After-Action Report is on the results screen. It has your overall grade stamped on an Army Form, every wave’s grade, and a superlative for every man in the section, from Crack Shot of the Day to Tea Boy and Bullet Magnet. Your best grade on each front goes on your Service Record, and the roll call (Tab) shows everyone’s Spirit.

The Last Round

New
  • Every wave now ends on a newsreel. The shot that kills the last man of the wave plays again in slow motion on everyone’s screen. The camera follows it from the muzzle, along the tracer, to the man. Bazooka rounds, grenades, satchels and fire missions get the same treatment. If a Panzer is the last to go, you see it go up again.
  • A caption says who did it, with what, and from how far: “PVT. ELLIS – KAR98K – 212 YDS”. A headshot gets the old dink and THROUGH THE HELMET. If you finish a wave during a Crack Shot, it’s billed as a Crack Shot.
  • It lasts about a second, and any key skips it. Your commendations wait until it’s finished. To turn it off, go to OPTIONS › Last round camera. In a section, the host’s setting is the one that counts.

The Sar’nt-Major is on the net

New

There’s a parade-ground voice on a crackly field radio now. He calls out the big moments.

  • Yours. He shouts HEADSHOT! for a run of heads, and HAT-TRICK! or FOUR OF A KIND! for a quick string of kills. There’s CRACK SHOT! too, and COUP DE GRACE! when a grenade you threw back kills the man who threw it.
  • The section’s. He calls STAND TO! with the wave’s number, WAVE HELD – WELL DONE, THE REARGUARD!, LAST MAN STANDING – GOOD LUCK, LAD! and FRONT SECURED! He’ll also warn you about a Tiger on the road, a Stuka diving or the post being breached.
  • Your mates’. Their big streaks get called with their callsign (“ELLIS – HAT-TRICK!”). Even with six of you he only calls one every ten seconds, and he never talks over himself or HQ.
  • Under OPTIONS › AUDIO you can turn him off, set his volume, or pick a British or American voice. If radio subtitles are on, his calls are written at the bottom of the screen too.

Anti-tank gun

New

Every front now has a 6-pounder dug in inside the wire. It’s a 37 mm on Henderson Field and a captured Pak 40 in the Factory District. Its shells come up with the first AT crate.

  • Press F at the gun to take a seat. The gunner aims and fires it, and R swaps between armour-piercing and high-explosive. Push past the stops and the whole trail heaves round.
  • A second soldier on the loader’s seat loads it in 1.5 s instead of 4. If you’re on your own, a squadmate comes over to load for you.
  • AP hits like a bazooka, and hitting a tank’s weak sides still counts. HE clears a knot of infantry and never hurts your own side.
  • Tanks go after a manned gun first. Two shells knock it out, and you hold F at it to dig it out again. Brew up armour with two of you on the gun and you both get the new GUN CREW mention.

More to draw

New cards
Quartermaster’s Issue · 9 new cards

Field Telephone

Ring up and ask for trouble. Next wave brings snipers, flamethrowers or a Stuka. Hold that wave and everyone’s next commendation is a tier better. Once a deployment.

Section cards

Captured MG42s, Mine Belt, Sniper Team, Wire Party

Captured MG42s puts an MG42 on a tripod at the post. Mine Belt lays mines across the ways in, and they won’t hurt your side. Sniper Team puts two GIs on the roof for three waves, and Wire Party lays a new belt of wire. A section card helps everyone once. Drawing it twice just tops up your pouches.

Per front

A friendly patrol

The Marines, the Resistance, the Airborne, Italian partisans or Commandos work the flank for a wave, and fewer riflemen arrive.

Your own

Smoke Grenades, Medic’s Bag, Morale Ration

Smoke Grenades makes your grenades lay smoke for three waves. Medic’s Bag makes revives faster and gets a man up at 75%. Morale Ration brings your health back sooner.

8 new Epic commendations

Action, not numbers

Rifle Grenade Rack, Double Tap, Incendiary Tracers, Stand Fast, Sapper’s Eye, Stout Heart, Snap Shot and Battle Cry.

Legendary pair

INFERNO

The flamethrower gets Legendaries of its own. Take Backdraft and Bottomless Tank together and you get a chain of fireballs that keeps the tank full.

Up the Rearguard!

New
  • Press N in a fight to stick your helmet on your muzzle and cheer. Each soldier who joins in within five seconds adds +5% reload speed for the whole section for ten seconds, up to +30% with six. You get one rally a wave. If everyone cheers, you all earn ALL TOGETHER.
  • Brew up. In a wave break the same key raises a mug of tea instead. It doesn’t do anything.
  • Re-enlisting is worth it now. Each Service Star puts a service chevron by your name and pays a re-enlistment bounty of +10% RP for your next 20 waves. At Star I a souvenir MP40 waits for you at the post, and at Star III a scoped Kar98k joins it. Star II has veterans’ orders (Head Hunters, Walking Wounded, Worn Kit) if you want it harder and better paid. There are ten new Veteran Traits, and Star V puts a gold frame round your name.

Bring your own men

Changed
  • In co-op, everyone now brings one of their own hired riflemen, not just the host. Your hires and your Platoon tree count in a friend’s game, so your man has the health, aim, issued weapon and field promotions you gave him.
  • With 1 to 3 soldiers the section fields up to 3 men. With 4 or 5 it’s 4, and a full section of six gets 5. The host’s first man goes first, then one for each friend in the order you joined.
  • Your man carries your name. He shows as YOURS on the roster and as “ELLIS’S MAN” under a friend’s crosshair. When you go down, he comes for you first. SQUAD STAYS BEHIND now works for everyone.
  • Big sections. With more than three soldiers, waves get denser, not longer. You get more men at once, and more paratroopers, snipers, half-track infantry and cavalry. With one to three soldiers it plays exactly as before.

Hold the line has a way out

Changed
  • CALL THE LORRIES. After every fifth wave past the relief, the section can vote to call the lorries. Then you hold the far gate for 90 seconds against everything they’ve got. At the minute, the lorries come up the track with their lamps lit.
  • Get aboard before they pull out. You can carry a wounded mate to the tailgate too: hold F on him and walk. If you get away, the run ends Evacuated with Honour. Your pay on the line goes up from 60% to 90%, and there’s a new decoration in it. If you’re overrun on the way out, it’s an ordinary overrun. You keep everything and only lose the bonus.
  • Ten new threats on the line. They turn up one at a time, each with a card: Wire Cutters, Sturm Company, Officer’s Staff, Sniper Screen, Rocket Mortars, Ghost Company, Flak Barrage (get under a roof), King Tiger and Kampfgruppe. Henderson Field gets a Banzai Company and a Tank Company. Night falls at wave ten past the relief.
  • Every fifth wave the radio gives you a job. It might be holding the crossroads, silencing the mortar pit or bringing back the dispatches. BRIEFING shows your best evacuation on each front.

We checked every map

Fixed
  • BRIEFING now shows the map before you deploy. You get its code and name, what’s different about it, today’s conditions and a small sketch of the ground. In a section, everyone sees the host’s map before pressing READY.
  • We built every front on 20,000 map codes and checked each one: every spawn, hiding place, mortar pit, MG nest, gate and vault.
  • Monte Lungo: mortar teams now set up down the terraces where you can see them from the wall. They used to hide in a spot nobody could reach. A mortar team hidden behind a hedge or in the jungle no longer counts as “in sight”.
  • Nobody spawns inside a bush, a cover box or a burnt-out truck any more, and the burnt trucks are in the same place for every player. Your machine guns have a clear lane of fire. The pine tree in front of the Ardennes gun is gone. An enemy who gets stuck on scenery is freed within seconds.
  • A few maps you kept from playtest 17 may look slightly different close to the camp.

Easier to see

Changed
  • Night isn’t pitch black any more. Night Attack, Full Moon and Last Light still feel like night, but you can see the men at the wire. The enemy can’t see any better than before. Flares burn longer and brighter.
  • There’s a new OPTIONS › VIDEO page with Brightness, “Night: keep it bright” (on by default) and Render scale. You can also set aim down sights to HOLD or TOGGLE, and menu text to 115% or 130%. In a friend’s game you can skip the host’s Crack Shot slow motion on your own screen.
  • We sorted out the HUD. Banners don’t stack on the wave title any more, and the combo sits just above the crosshair. Nothing runs into anything at 720p or at 130% HUD size. Blitz, Molten Barrel, Pinpoint and friends show a small timer over your weapon list.
  • The BRIEFING tells you when the new conditions arrive, in your local time. The board never changes in the middle of a deployment.

Report from the field

New
  • If something breaks, looks wrong or feels unfair, press F10 (or REPORT A PROBLEM in the pause menu, or REPORT at HQ). Write a line or two about what happened and press SEND. It goes straight to #feedback in the RearGuard Discord.
  • A picture of the screen from the moment you pressed it goes too. So do the front, wave, map code, conditions and Honours, so you don’t have to copy “Map 4F2A” down by hand. Untick the picture if you’d rather not send it. To send a suggestion instead, tick “Something I’d change”.
  • It never sends your chat or anyone’s address. If it can’t connect, the report is kept and offered again from the pause menu. You can send one report a minute.
  • The War Diary. RECORDS › WAR DIARY now writes up every deployment, a line a day: where you held, for how long, and who was with you.

Fixes

Fixed
  • If one player’s game crashes, it no longer drops the whole section. Before, one client crashing hard took every other client with it, and then the host wouldn’t let anyone new join. Now only the one who crashed leaves, and someone new can join.
  • Your service record is now backed up. The game keeps the last few saves next to it and writes each new one safely, so a crash or power cut in the middle of saving can’t wipe your progress any more. If a save is ever damaged, the game loads the most recent good backup and the Briefing tells you. The damaged file is kept, never written over.
  • Old builds can’t join a new host any more. Instead of being dropped without a word, you get a plain message telling you to update. The post is full at six.
  • If you join mid-run, you now see the mass assault, the runners and any Stuka already in the air. You also get a commendation offer for each wave already held, up to five.
  • The stutter of about a second at the start of a wave, the first time cavalry rode in, is gone. So are the shorter stutters the first time you heard a flamethrower, a rocket battery or a Stuka. Players who join no longer freeze when the first horses appear.
  • Half-track squads and tank escorts get out beside their vehicle instead of being pushed away from it.
  • Developer switches no longer work in the release game.

What we’d like you to test

  • No person has played this build yet. Press F10 the moment something looks wrong. It sends the map code for you.
  • The Sar’nt-Major: too chatty, too quiet, or about right? Which voice?
  • The Last Round cam: does it earn its second every wave, or do you start skipping it?
  • The aces: can you read each one’s plan? Is Adler too passive once his mortars run out, and are the riders a good target or a cheap one?
  • Fire Plan: did the Doodlebug going quiet get you? Were the Goliaths a puzzle or a nuisance in a full section?
  • Spirit and grades: does a rifle player who only takes heads feel short-changed?
  • The anti-tank gun: does a tank turning on your gun feel thrilling or punishing? Does the gun do enough from the front?
  • Night: too bright now? You can turn “keep it bright” off in VIDEO and tell us which you prefer.
  • Hold the line: would you call the lorries at five, or push on? Did anyone get carried aboard?
  • Bring your own men: did your friend’s man show up with the right kit, and did he come for you when you went down?
