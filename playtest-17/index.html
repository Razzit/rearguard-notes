The enemy has learned fieldcraft

Changed

Riflemen no longer walk into the open and stop. They come on as squads, move from cover to cover, and dig in just outside the wire.

Squads

Bounding in pairs

Men who arrive together form a squad with its own lane. One pair dashes to the next cover while the other kneels and fires, then they swap. They stop on a firing line a few metres outside the sandbags and shoot from there.

Your moment

They stand to shoot

A man kneels in cover and stands up for about a second before each shot. Rounds cracking past him, or a .30 cal working the line, put him to ground, shooting wild.

Grenades

They run from them

Men near a landed grenade scatter. Cook it and they won’t get far. Bayonet charges, sappers and flamethrowers still come straight for the gates.

Over the top

Vaulting the sandbags

Shock troops charge across the fields and climb the wall instead of queueing at the gates. For a moment each one is on top of the sandbags against the sky. Take the shot. On Monte Lungo they climb in through the houses’ low windows.

Movement

Roads, hedges and tanks

Men march in along the tracks and go round hedges and craters instead of through them. Armoured cars, half-tracks and tanks plan routes they actually fit and keep to the hill road and the beach road.

New threats

New enemies
Battlefield event · from wave 4

Stuka attack

The siren builds, a red line is painted where its guns will walk and a ring where the bomb lands. Get off the line or under a roof. Or shoot it down: a .30 cal on a nest counts double and a half. Send it home smoking for the ACE accolade. On Henderson Field it’s a Val.

Later waves, earlier on hard fronts

Nebelwerfer battery

A six-barrel rocket launcher sets up far out. When you hear the moan, clear the red ring. Snipe the crew while they stand up to reload, or blow the glowing rocket stack for COUNTER-BATTERY.

Final assault

The Tiger

Leads the final assault on Factory District (the Chi-Nu on Henderson Field) and on any front from Battle Honours IV. Its 88 flattens sandbags and kills a man in the open. Rockets on its front plate only half count. Get behind it.

Wave 10 and up, German fronts

Sturmpioniere

Assault engineers come behind smoke with the heavy gunner and blow a hole in the sandbags. Sandbags can be breached now. Engineers hold E at a gap to fill it, the Sandbag Detail card mends every gap, and the squad fills them when a wave is held.

Henderson Field

Banzai!

An officer with a sword and a bugle now leads every charge in, and his men go for the nearest defender anywhere on the field. Kill him and the charge breaks.

Counter-attacks

New

From wave 4, HQ sometimes orders an optional job outside the wire for the next wave. Listen for the radio, read the card under the wave count, follow the green marker.

Silence the mortar pit

A dug-in mortar and its rifle guards are shelling the post. Go out and finish the crew.

Blow the MG nest

Grab the satchel charge at the command post, plant it at the nest (hold E) and run. Five-second fuse. Shooting the crew instead only earns half.

Recover the dispatches

Fetch a fallen dispatch rider’s map case and walk it back inside the wire. No running, and Jerry knows you have it.

Hold the crossroads

Keep a man inside the taped ring for a minute while they push for it.

Win before the wave is beaten and the whole section earns requisition, XP, a bonus commendation at the next break and the SALLY FORTH accolade. Fail and it costs nothing. New decoration: the Raiding Party Clasp for ten counter-attacks won.

Conditions and the Weekly Operation

New

Every front on the deploy board now carries up to two Conditions for the day. The board is the same for everyone and changes daily, and there is always one front with clear conditions.

Warnings · pay more

Harder

Night Attack, Fog, Snowstorm, Rationed Ammunition, Veteran Sappers, Double Bounties, Crack Troops, Dawn Attack.

Anomalies · just different

Different

Rain, Full Moon, Last Light, Quiet Front, Captured Stores, Fresh Reinforcements.

Night attacks

Flares

Flares go up over the wire every minute or so, and a Radioman can send one up with his field glasses. Their riflemen spot you later in the dark, but your muzzle flash gives you away.

All week

The Weekly Operation

One set front, two conditions and a fixed Battle Honours tier for everyone, all week. Play it solo or together and post your best wave on the board. Hold the front on four operations for the Operations Clasp.

Conditions show on the briefing, beside the wave counter and on the telegram. In co-op the host’s board applies to the whole section. New soldiers get a clear board for their first two deployments.

Hold the line

New mode

Hold a front until relieved, then choose to stay. The waves keep coming with no second relief until the post falls, and everything you earned banks.

  • Every wave on the line brings something new: a second MG42 team, a Heavy Gunner squad, mortars in pairs, double armour, paratroopers and cavalry together. Intelligence tells you what’s coming in the break.
  • Mass assaults. Every 5th wave on the line, and every front’s final assault, the bugle sounds and the fields fill with running men. Riflemen can drop them at long range before they reach the wire.
  • Records. Your best on each front and difficulty is kept with your name and the date and shown on the BRIEFING page. The Rearguard Medal is for holding 5, 10 or 15 waves past the relief, and STILL STANDING comes every 5 waves.
  • Last stand. When the post is nearly gone, flares go up and the squad fixes bayonets.
  • In co-op the host decides; vote with the ready key on the citation. Pay on the line is 60%, so it’s for glory, not farming. You can hold one extra Legendary once you stay.

No two deployments alike

New
  • Every deployment rolls a map. The command post, wire, buildings, terraces and roads stay where you learned them, but hedgerows, copses, wrecks, craters, rubble, pines and undergrowth fall differently each time.
  • Two versions of every front: Hedgerow Farm’s Orchard or Sunken Lane, the Ardennes Clearing or Deep Forest, Monte Lungo’s Vineyard Terraces or Rockfall, the Factory’s Rail Yard or Collapsed Hall, Henderson Field’s Creek Crossing or Burnt Plantation.
  • Know your map. The intro, the roll call (Tab) and the telegram name it (“Map 4F2A, the Sunken Lane”). Quote it when you report a bug.
  • Familiar ground. Liked a map? Press KEEP THIS MAP on the telegram, then SELECT it on the BRIEFING page to fight there again. In co-op everyone fights on the host’s map.

The battlefield looks and sounds new

Changed
Ground

Real ground

No more grass repeating every few steps. Fields fade lighter and darker, tracks and craters have ragged edges, and the open ground gently rolls. Grass grows round the camp.

Plants

Trees made of leaves

Oaks, apple trees, bushes and bocage hedges have a leafy outline that sways and glows against the sun. Snowy spruces in the Ardennes, olives on Monte Lungo, broad leaves at Henderson, dead weeds in the Factory.

Soldiers

Faces and kit

Enemies and your squad have faces, chinstraps, netted helmets, canteens and spades, mud on their knees, and rolled sleeves on summer fronts. They turn their rifles to what they’re shooting at, kick with every shot, flinch when hit and crumple when they fall.

Fire

Flashes, brass and marks

Muzzle flashes sized to the gun, spent brass that tinks on the ground, glowing tracers, bigger blasts. The field keeps the marks: bullet holes in walls and timber, scorched earth, blood where men fall.

Sound

A battlefield, not a range

Rifles and MGs across the field come to you as a distant crack and a rolling echo, a little after the flash. Your own shots slap back off walls and treelines; step into the post and the fight outside goes dull. Rounds sound different on wood, stone, sheet metal, sandbags, mud and leaves, and your footsteps change with the ground.

Ambience

Wind, birds, crickets

Every front has its own wind, a distant battle that swells as a wave presses in, birds that go silent when the shooting starts, and crickets at night.

Guns

New & changed
Rank 24, or the Flame Section card

The M2 flamethrower

A flame crate lands by the post every 4th wave once anyone in the section has rank 24, or draw it from the Quartermaster’s Issue. Hold fire for a roaring cone out to 14 m. Men caught in it burn and break; past 14 m it does nothing, so let them come to the wire. Two tanks, on the bazooka’s key 4. Hold it on an enemy flamethrower and his tank goes up. Never burns your own side. New cards Long Burn and Napalm Mix, new accolade ROASTED.

Rank 10 · key H

Satchel Charge

A new Quartermaster’s Issue card: one charge a wave, three-second fuse. It sticks to vehicles and wrecks them.

Gunsmith

Pistol and Lee-Enfield

The issue M1911 gets a Lanyard Ring, Painted Sights, a 10-round magazine and a Match Barrel, plus three cards of its own. The Lee-Enfield gets a full gunsmith: sling, ladder sight, charger drill, spike bayonet, flash eliminator, jungle barrel, cheek rest, No. 32 scope and a cup discharger for rifle grenades.

Feel

Rifles that feel like rifles

Bolt-actions are worked in view and the sights come back on target the moment you can fire again (prefer the old way? OPTIONS › Working the bolt › STAY AIMED). The Garand pings. The Trench Gun loads shell by shell and the trigger cuts the reload short. Aiming snaps in and settles, every shot kicks the gun the way your aim went, and the gun comes up out of a sprint. Hold R with a full magazine to inspect your gun. A “Weapon motion” slider turns it all down if it makes you queasy.

No weapon’s damage, fire rate, recoil or spread numbers have changed.

Arcade

New
  • CRACK SHOT. Chain three headshot kills, drop a man at 70 m or more, or take an officer or sniper through the head, and the whole section’s world slows for a held breath. Keep landing heads to stretch it. Turn it off under OPTIONS › GAMEPLAY; in co-op the host decides.
  • Combos. Hit one man with three different kinds of hurt inside two seconds (head, fire, shock, blast, steel, bayonet, pinned, booby trap) for a COMBO and a bigger bounty if he falls in it. Five kinds earns a KNOCKOUT BLOW.
  • Watch the section. Killed in co-op? Ride along over a teammate’s shoulder until the wave is held. Click or A/D to switch, and you can still ping.
  • Incoming fire. MG42 bursts and heavy fire crack past you, kick dust at your feet and close in the edges of your view, then clear fast.
  • Legendary pairs on the cards. Every pair Legendary shows its partner and the pair it makes, and gets a brass COMPLETES THE PAIR tag when you hold the other half. The Tab sheet tracks half-finished pairs.
  • Headshot kills get their own crisp hit marker and a tiny hit-stop. BLOODY NOSE: the section’s first kill of each wave gets a sting.

Your squad fights like a section

Changed
  • They revive you. In co-op, a squadmate within 20 m of a downed soldier runs over, kneels and gets him up (6 s, quicker with a Medic in the deployment). He won’t try with the enemy on top of you, and a hit sets him back.
  • They move. They shift to face the main thrust before the first shot, go to ground under heavy fire, get clear of grenades, and fall back into the post’s room when it’s nearly gone and the wire is breached.
  • They talk. “Sapper at the east gate!”, “Armour! North-east!”, “Grenade!”, “Man the gun!” The section no longer talks over itself.
  • Follow me. Host: hold the ping key (Q) and your squad follows you out, up to 25 m past the wire. Let go to have them hold, or let go inside the wire to send them back to their posts.
  • Field promotions show as gold chevrons on the roster and over the man’s head, and the roster shows what each man is doing. Veterans pick their shots: officers, sappers and flamethrowers first, aimed head shots on men standing still.

Rank rewards that actually reward

Changed
  • The commendation pool grows at ranks 4, 9, 16 and 24. New soldiers see a smaller, clearer set; veterans unlock Ricochet, Hot Potato, Booby Traps, Shoulder Charge and more. The promotion rows name the cards.
  • Specialist level rewards are real: 25 finishes, shoulder patches, helmet stencils and calling cards to wear, from the Red Cross Brassard to the Brass-Cased Handset.
  • Ranks 33 to 49 each add a lasting bonus: more RP, a spare reroll, an extra Issue card, all seven attachment slots, a third Legendary, and more. You still get the rank’s cosmetic and pay.
  • Everything is per soldier in co-op: your rank opens your rewards, whoever is hosting.

New players and options

New
  • New soldiers start as Replacements: fewer enemies who hit softer, and no special troops until wave 5. It pays a little less, you can switch it off under the front’s difficulty, and it ends once you hold Hedgerow Farm.
  • First-deployment tips, one line at a time with the key, as each moment comes up. The Field Manual now opens on a one-page “How a deployment goes”.
  • Range Course: six stations against the clock with bronze, silver and gold times. Gold wins the Expert Infantryman Badge.
  • Accessibility: colour-blind marker colours (three sets), radio and squad subtitles, and HUD size up to 130%. The fronts list now recommends a front and says why.

Fixes

Fixed
  • The “Commendation pool +2”, “Satchel Charge” and “flamethrower loan” rank rewards now exist. Before, they did nothing.
  • Muzzle flashes, tracers and sounds no longer create objects every shot, and the game mixes up to 64 sounds at once without the near ones being cut off.
  • Fewer draw calls on every front; cover and hiding places are exactly where they were.
  • Men wedged in a knot at a gate squeeze past instead of standing there all wave. Your squad no longer stands stuck at a gate.
  • Vehicles no longer get stuck between cottages and hedges.
  • In co-op, HOLD THE LINE pay takes the same cut as solo.
  • The Nebelwerfer and counter-attacks work on Monte Lungo, and a wave can no longer hang waiting for a battery that never arrived.

What we’d like you to test

  • This is a big build and none of it has been played by a person yet. If something looks wrong, say the front, the wave and the map code from the roll call.
  • The enemy’s fieldcraft: do the firefights at the wire feel fair? Are squads too slow to close, or too fast?
  • Stuka and Nebelwerfer: can you read them coming? Is the Tiger too much on Factory District?
  • Counter-attacks: is the pay worth leaving the post? Which job is the best fun?
  • Night and fog: too dark, too thick, or about right on your screen?
  • Sound: is the enemy’s fire loud enough at range, and does the late crack feel right or laggy?
  • Crack Shot: does the slow-mo feel good at three headshots, or does it come too often?
  • Guns: the bolt in view, the sprint-out, the sway. Tell us if any of it fights you.
  • Hold the line: how far did you get? Did the mass assault feel like one?
