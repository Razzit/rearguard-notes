The enemy has learned fieldcraft

Changed

Riflemen don’t walk into the open and stop any more. They come in as squads, move from cover to cover and dig in just outside the wire.

Squads

Bounding in pairs

Men who arrive together form a squad with its own lane. One pair dashes to the next cover while the other kneels and fires, then they swap. They stop on a firing line a few metres outside the sandbags and shoot from there.

Your moment

They stand to shoot

A man kneels in cover and stands up for about a second before each shot. Put rounds past him, or work the line with a .30 cal, and he goes to ground and shoots wild.

Grenades

They run from them

Men scatter from a grenade once it lands. Cook it and they won’t get far. Bayonet charges, sappers and flamethrowers still come straight for the gates.

Over the top

Vaulting the sandbags

Shock troops charge across the fields and climb the wall instead of queueing at the gates. Each one is up on the sandbags against the sky for a moment, and that’s when to shoot him. On Monte Lungo they climb in through the low windows of the houses.

Movement

Roads, hedges and tanks

Men march in along the tracks and go round hedges and craters, not through them. Armoured cars, half-tracks and tanks pick routes they actually fit through, and stick to the hill road and the beach road.

New threats

New enemies
Battlefield event · from wave 4

Stuka attack

You’ll hear the siren build. A red line shows where its guns will walk, and a ring shows where the bomb will land. Get off the line or get under a roof. You can also shoot it down, and a .30 cal on a nest counts double and a half. Send it home smoking and you get the ACE accolade. On Henderson Field it’s a Val.

Later waves, earlier on hard fronts

Nebelwerfer battery

A six-barrel rocket launcher sets up a long way out. When you hear the moan, get out of the red ring. Snipe the crew while they stand up to reload, or blow the glowing rocket stack for COUNTER-BATTERY.

Final assault

The Tiger

It leads the final assault on Factory District, and on any front from Battle Honours IV. On Henderson Field it’s the Chi-Nu. Its 88 flattens sandbags and kills a man in the open. Rockets on its front plate only count for half, so get behind it.

Wave 10 and up, German fronts

Sturmpioniere

Assault engineers come in behind smoke with the heavy gunner and blow a hole in the sandbags. Sandbags can be breached now. Engineers hold E at a gap to fill it. The Sandbag Detail card mends every gap, and the squad fills them in when you hold a wave.

Henderson Field

Banzai!

An officer with a sword and a bugle now leads every charge in, and his men go for the nearest defender anywhere on the field. Kill him and the charge breaks.

Counter-attacks

New

From wave 4, HQ will sometimes give you an optional job outside the wire for the next wave. You’ll hear it on the radio. The card under the wave count says what it is, and a green marker shows you where.

Silence the mortar pit

A dug-in mortar and its rifle guards are shelling the post. Go out and finish the crew.

Blow the MG nest

Grab the satchel charge at the post, plant it at the nest (hold E) and run. It’s a five-second fuse. If you shoot the crew instead, you only get half.

Recover the dispatches

Fetch a fallen dispatch rider’s map case and walk it back inside the wire. You can’t run with it, and Jerry knows you’ve got it.

Hold the crossroads

Keep a man inside the taped ring for a minute while they push for it.

If you win it before the wave is beaten, the whole section gets requisition, XP, a bonus commendation at the next break and the SALLY FORTH accolade. Failing costs nothing. There’s a new decoration too, the Raiding Party Clasp, for winning ten counter-attacks.

Conditions and the Weekly Operation

New

Each front on the deploy board now has up to two Conditions for the day. Everyone gets the same board and it changes daily. There’s always one front with clear conditions.

Warnings · pay more

Harder

Night Attack, Fog, Snowstorm, Rationed Ammunition, Veteran Sappers, Double Bounties, Crack Troops, Dawn Attack.

Anomalies · just different

Different

Rain, Full Moon, Last Light, Quiet Front, Captured Stores, Fresh Reinforcements.

Night attacks

Flares

Flares go up over the wire every minute or so, and a Radioman can send one up with his field glasses. In the dark their riflemen are slower to spot you, but your muzzle flash gives you away.

All week

The Weekly Operation

Everyone gets the same front, two conditions and a fixed Battle Honours tier for the whole week. Play it solo or together and post your best wave on the board. Hold the front on four operations and you get the Operations Clasp.

Conditions show on the briefing, beside the wave counter and on the telegram. In co-op the host’s board applies to the whole section. New soldiers get a clear board for their first two deployments.

Hold the line

New mode

Once you’re relieved on a front, you can choose to stay. The waves keep coming and there’s no second relief, so it runs until the post falls. Everything you earned still banks.

  • Every wave on the line brings something new. It might be a second MG42 team, a Heavy Gunner squad, mortars in pairs, double armour, or paratroopers and cavalry together. Intelligence tells you what’s coming in the break.
  • Mass assaults. Every 5th wave on the line, and every front’s final assault, the bugle sounds and the fields fill with running men. Riflemen can drop them at long range before they reach the wire.
  • Records. We keep your best on each front and difficulty, with your name and the date, and show it on the BRIEFING page. You get the Rearguard Medal for holding 5, 10 or 15 waves past the relief, and STILL STANDING every 5 waves.
  • Last stand. When the post is nearly gone, flares go up and the squad fixes bayonets.
  • In co-op the host decides, and you vote with the ready key on the citation. Pay on the line is 60%, so it’s for glory, not farming. Once you stay, you can hold one extra Legendary.

No two deployments alike

New
  • Every deployment rolls a map. The post, wire, buildings, terraces and roads stay where you learned them. Hedgerows, copses, wrecks, craters, rubble, pines and undergrowth land differently each time.
  • Every front has two versions. Hedgerow Farm has the Orchard or the Sunken Lane. The Ardennes has the Clearing or the Deep Forest. Monte Lungo has the Vineyard Terraces or Rockfall, the Factory has the Rail Yard or the Collapsed Hall, and Henderson Field has the Creek Crossing or the Burnt Plantation.
  • Know your map. The intro, the roll call (Tab) and the telegram all name it, like “Map 4F2A, the Sunken Lane”. Put that in when you report a bug.
  • Familiar ground. Liked a map? Press KEEP THIS MAP on the telegram, then SELECT it on the BRIEFING page to fight there again. In co-op everyone fights on the host’s map.

The battlefield looks and sounds new

Changed
Ground

Real ground

The grass doesn’t repeat every few steps any more. Fields go lighter and darker in patches, tracks and craters have ragged edges, and the open ground rolls a little. There’s grass growing round the camp too.

Plants

Trees made of leaves

Oaks, apple trees, bushes and bocage hedges have a leafy outline that sways, and it glows with the sun behind it. The Ardennes has snowy spruces and Monte Lungo has olives. Henderson gets broad leaves and the Factory gets dead weeds.

Soldiers

Faces and kit

Enemies and your squad have faces now, with chinstraps, netted helmets, canteens and spades. They’ve got mud on their knees, and their sleeves are rolled up on summer fronts. They point their rifles at what they’re shooting at and the rifle kicks with every shot. They flinch when they’re hit and crumple when they fall.

Fire

Flashes, brass and marks

Muzzle flashes are sized to the gun. Spent brass tinks on the ground, tracers glow and the blasts are bigger. The marks stay on the field, too. You’ll see bullet holes in walls and timber, scorched earth, and blood where men fall.

Sound

A battlefield, not a range

Rifles and MGs across the field sound like a distant crack and a rolling echo, and you hear them a little after the flash. Your own shots slap back off walls and treelines. Step into the post and the fight outside goes dull. Rounds sound different on wood, stone, sheet metal, sandbags, mud and leaves, and your footsteps change with the ground.

Ambience

Wind, birds, crickets

Every front has its own wind. There’s a distant battle that gets louder as a wave pushes in, birds that go quiet when the shooting starts, and crickets at night.

Guns

New & changed
Rank 24, or the Flame Section card

The M2 flamethrower

Once anyone in the section has rank 24, a flame crate lands by the post every 4th wave. You can also draw it from the Quartermaster’s Issue. Hold fire and it throws a roaring cone out to 14 m. Men caught in it burn and break. Past 14 m it does nothing, so let them come to the wire. You get two tanks, and it sits on the bazooka’s key 4. Hold it on an enemy flamethrower and his tank goes up. It never burns your own side. There are two new cards, Long Burn and Napalm Mix, and a new accolade, ROASTED.

Rank 10 · key H

Satchel Charge

This is a new Quartermaster’s Issue card. You get one charge a wave, on a three-second fuse. It sticks to vehicles and wrecks them.

Gunsmith

Pistol and Lee-Enfield

The issue M1911 gets a Lanyard Ring, Painted Sights, a 10-round magazine and a Match Barrel, plus three cards of its own. The Lee-Enfield gets a full gunsmith: sling, ladder sight, charger drill, spike bayonet, flash eliminator, jungle barrel, cheek rest, No. 32 scope and a cup discharger for rifle grenades.

Feel

Rifles that feel like rifles

You see the bolt worked now, and the sights come back on target the moment you can fire again. If you’d rather have the old way, it’s OPTIONS › Working the bolt › STAY AIMED. The Garand pings. The Trench Gun loads shell by shell, and pulling the trigger cuts the reload short. Aiming snaps in and settles. Every shot kicks the gun the way your aim went, and the gun comes up when you come out of a sprint. Hold R with a full magazine to inspect your gun. If any of it makes you queasy, the “Weapon motion” slider turns it all down.

We haven’t changed any weapon’s damage, fire rate, recoil or spread numbers.

Arcade

New
  • CRACK SHOT. Time slows for the whole section for a held breath when you chain three headshot kills, drop a man at 70 m or more, or get an officer or sniper in the head. Keep landing headshots to stretch it out. You can turn it off under OPTIONS › GAMEPLAY. In co-op the host decides.
  • Combos. Hit one man with three different kinds of damage inside two seconds and it’s a COMBO. If he goes down inside it, the bounty is bigger. The kinds are head, fire, shock, blast, steel, bayonet, pinned and booby trap. Five kinds is a KNOCKOUT BLOW.
  • Watch the section. If you’re killed in co-op, you ride along over a teammate’s shoulder until the wave is held. Click or press A/D to switch between them. You can still ping.
  • Incoming fire. MG42 bursts and heavy fire crack past you, kick dust at your feet and close in the edges of your view, then clear fast.
  • Legendary pairs on the cards. Each pair Legendary shows its partner and the pair they make. It gets a brass COMPLETES THE PAIR tag when you already hold the other half. The Tab sheet tracks half-finished pairs.
  • Headshot kills get their own crisp hit marker and a tiny hit-stop. BLOODY NOSE is a sting for the section’s first kill of each wave.

Your squad fights like a section

Changed
  • They revive you. In co-op, a squadmate within 20 m of a downed soldier will run over, kneel and get him up. It takes 6 s, or less with a Medic in the deployment. He won’t try it with the enemy on top of you, and getting hit sets him back.
  • They move. Before the first shot they turn to face where the main push is coming from. They go to ground under heavy fire and get clear of grenades. When the post is nearly gone and the wire is breached, they fall back into its room.
  • They talk. “Sapper at the east gate!”, “Armour! North-east!”, “Grenade!”, “Man the gun!” And they don’t talk over each other any more.
  • Follow me. If you’re the host, hold the ping key (Q) and your squad follows you out, up to 25 m past the wire. Let go and they hold where they are. Let go inside the wire and they go back to their posts.
  • Field promotions show as gold chevrons on the roster and over the man’s head. The roster also shows what each man is doing. Veterans pick their shots. They go for officers, sappers and flamethrowers first, and take aimed head shots at men standing still.

Rank rewards that actually reward

Changed
  • The commendation pool grows at ranks 4, 9, 16 and 24. New soldiers see a smaller, clearer set. Veterans unlock Ricochet, Hot Potato, Booby Traps, Shoulder Charge and more. The promotion rows name the cards.
  • Specialist level rewards are real now. There are 25 finishes, shoulder patches, helmet stencils and calling cards to wear, from the Red Cross Brassard to the Brass-Cased Handset.
  • Ranks 33 to 49 each add a lasting bonus. That’s more RP, a spare reroll, an extra Issue card, all seven attachment slots, a third Legendary and so on. You still get the rank’s cosmetic and pay.
  • In co-op it’s all per soldier. Your rank opens your rewards, whoever is hosting.

New players and options

New
  • New soldiers start as Replacements. There are fewer enemies, they hit softer, and there are no special troops until wave 5. It pays a little less. You can switch it off under the front’s difficulty, and it ends once you hold Hedgerow Farm.
  • First-deployment tips show one line at a time, with the key, as each moment comes up. The Field Manual now opens on a one-page “How a deployment goes”.
  • Range Course. Six stations against the clock, with bronze, silver and gold times. Gold wins the Expert Infantryman Badge.
  • Accessibility. You get three sets of colour-blind marker colours, plus subtitles for the radio and the squad. The HUD goes up to 130%. The fronts list now recommends a front and says why.

Fixes

Fixed
  • The “Commendation pool +2”, “Satchel Charge” and “flamethrower loan” rank rewards did nothing before. They work now.
  • Muzzle flashes, tracers and sounds don’t create objects every shot any more. The game mixes up to 64 sounds at once without cutting off the near ones.
  • Every front uses fewer draw calls. Cover and hiding places are exactly where they were.
  • Men jammed in a knot at a gate now squeeze past instead of standing there all wave. Your squad doesn’t get stuck at gates any more.
  • Vehicles no longer get stuck between cottages and hedges.
  • In co-op, HOLD THE LINE pay takes the same cut as solo.
  • The Nebelwerfer and counter-attacks work on Monte Lungo, and a wave can no longer hang waiting for a battery that never arrived.

What we’d like you to test

  • This is a big build and nobody has played any of it yet. If something looks wrong, tell us the front, the wave and the map code from the roll call.
  • Do the firefights at the wire feel fair now the enemy uses cover? Are squads too slow to close, or too fast?
  • Stuka and Nebelwerfer: can you read them coming? Is the Tiger too much on Factory District?
  • Counter-attacks: is the pay worth leaving the post? Which job is the best fun?
  • On your screen, are night and fog too dark, too thick, or about right?
  • Is the enemy’s fire loud enough at range? Does the late crack feel right, or does it feel like lag?
  • Crack Shot: does the slow-mo feel good at three headshots, or does it come too often?
  • Guns: tell us if the bolt in view, the sprint-out or the sway gets in your way.
  • How far did you get when you held the line? Did the mass assault feel like one?
